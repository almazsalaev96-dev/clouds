# Vision — Learning-outcomes first — the product as a learning and exam-results machine

## 1. One-paragraph thesis (what this product is, in your lens's terms)

This product is a **mark-producing learning system**, not a chatbot with a study toggle. Its atom is the *Attempt* — a student's unaided response to a syllabus-tagged, mark-scheme-backed item — and everything else exists to generate more good Attempts and extract more learning per Attempt: an examiner-calibrated marker that grades per Assessment Objective with quoted evidence and a published agreement number [A3][A19][A22]; a tutor whose every turn is governed by the help ladder, the effort gate and a no-sycophancy lint [A9][A10][A13]; a learner model that stores mastery per syllabus point on an FSRS clock anchored to the exam date [A48][A52]; and a planner that spends the student's hours where the expected marks per hour are highest. The pedagogical evidence is unambiguous about the two failure modes we are building against — the tutor that quietly does the work (Bastani: unguarded GPT +48% practice, *worse* exam; guarded tutor +127% practice, no harm [B]) and the marker that flatters ("ChatGPT predicts me like 16 marks and in reality I'll get 3" [A3]). The exam-board evidence is equally unambiguous about what earns marks: the AO the command word unlocks, the number of links in the chain of analysis, application that survives the cover-the-business-name test, a committed judgement in context [GM#B1.2][GM#B1.6]. We win by being the only product in which the learning science and the examiner's craft are the *same* machine: retrieval, spacing, interleaving, feedback and calibration are not features bolted onto chat — they are how the exam engine schedules, marks and debriefs. The claim we will make, and pre-register, is modest and falsifiable: students who use it as designed gain durable, transferable mastery and higher component marks than matched peers using frontier chat study modes — a 0.3–0.7 SD class of effect, never "2×" [A7].

## 2. Positioning: the honest answer to "why would a student use this instead of ChatGPT / Gemini / Claude (with their free study modes) + Save My Exams?" — 8–12 defensible differentiators, each tied to evidence [cite]

The honest framing: **they know everything; we know your exam — and we can prove it.** Every lab study mode is free, one click from off, and concedes it "may give a direct answer" [A1]; Save My Exams and a dozen CAIE micro-apps now claim "AI marking against the mark scheme" but *none publishes accuracy, none cites the source of a piece of feedback* [GL#0 Part B]. The differentiators below are ordered by defensibility (how hard they are for a lab to copy with a prompt).

1. **A marker as consistent as a second examiner, with the number published per paper.** Frontier models already reach or beat the examiner–examiner QWK line (0.84 in GCSE maths; +0.06 in science on 32,534 double-marked real scripts) but are more lenient than trained examiners and drift run-to-run [GM#0.1][A22][A23]. We add what a prompt cannot: gold sets built from Example Candidate Responses and teacher double-marked scripts, standardisation before release, 2–5% live seeding with auto-pause, dual-family ensemble on essays, and a public calibration page per paper [GM#A4.4][A22]. Students' #1 pain is lenient, inconsistent marking [A3]; nobody else has an answer that is a *statistic* rather than a slogan.
2. **Marks per AO, with quoted spans, following the board's own procedure.** Cambridge best-fit per AO grid, "developed analysis = two or more links in the chain", "repeating the business name is not application" — encoded as rules with evidence quotas, not left to model taste [A19][GM#B1.2][GM#Impl 2]. A student sees *which sentence* capped AO4 at Level 1 and the exact move that earns Level 3 — the feedback students explicitly ask for ("explain why this argument gets 2 points and not 3" [SV wish 4]).
3. **Effort gate and help ladder that produce exam gains, not practice inflation.** The only large RCT of unguarded chat in exam preparation found it harms the closed-book exam; the guard-railed version removed the harm [A9][B Bastani]. Off-the-shelf models leak the answer in the opening turn >96% of the time; multi-turn failures rise 17.7% → 77.8% [A17][B SafeTutors]. Ours is enforced by an orchestrator vetting every draft turn and 30-turn adversarial evals gating every release — a lab study mode is a prompt the student can switch off.
4. **A syllabus-code Course, not a chat.** No lab product maps an answer to a syllabus point, AO, command word or mark-scheme line, and Study Mode cannot even run inside Projects [A2]. The Course carries the curriculum pack (papers, AO weights per paper, command words, numbered content tree, thresholds) [A29], so "explain elasticity" is answered at 9708 depth with 9708 vocabulary, and "is this in the syllabus?" has a citable answer [A36].
5. **The tutor has prepared for the lesson.** Every positive AI-tutoring RCT gave the tutor a verified key [A18]; critics call Study Mode "a tutor that didn't prep for the session" [SV wish 17]. We pre-solve every item with a strong model plus SymPy verification and never grade an exam-style item without the scheme in context [A18][A51].
6. **A learner model that persists and decays honestly.** Students build spreadsheets "for each specification point to note when I last revised it" [SV wish 7]; ChatGPT degrades after ~40–50 messages and forgets between sessions [A48]. We keep mastery per syllabus point, misconceptions, seen-solution items and calibration outside the chat and re-inject them, with FSRS-6 scheduling whose desired retention ramps 0.85 → 0.95 as the exam approaches [A52].
7. **Feedback in the examiner's language, pointing at the highest-yield lost mark first.** Feedback works through information content (d=0.48), and person-praise is among the weakest forms [A13]; examiner reports repeat the same criticisms for years ("very limited evaluative sections") [GM#B1.6]. Each "next mark" tip is phrased as ERs phrase it and cites the ER [GM#Impl 12]; praise not tied to a specific correct action is linted to <5% of turns [A13].
8. **Misconception diagnosis, not re-explanation.** Every syllabus point ships with a misconception list mined from examiner reports and diagnostic distractors mapped to it [A15]; the tutor asks for the reasoning trace, classifies slip / misconception / missing prerequisite, and routes accordingly — while a chatbot re-explains the same passage with the same analogy [SV wish 16].
9. **A predicted grade computed the way the board computes it.** Raw → component threshold → PUM → option grade, as a range across recent series with "marks to next grade", A* only at option level [A24]. Chat products hallucinate boundaries; notes sites do not model grades at all [GL#0].
10. **Durable learning is measured, not assumed.** Confidence ratings on gradable items, predict–postdict–compare on every mock (calibration training g≈0.57), overconfident-wrong items re-scheduled at ×0.5, a cognitive-debt guard with graduated non-punitive responses that is A/B-tested on 28-day retention [A14][A16]. The north-star is unaided delayed performance, not minutes [A61].
11. **Integrity the school can endorse.** Never produces submittable coursework; every session leaves an exportable learning receipt — 64% of US high-schoolers fear false accusation, 58% of UK teachers now catch unauthorised AI use [A59][B]. Teachers get attempts-and-hints visibility, not detectors [A55].
12. **Honest limits and a free tier that survives May.** Published limits with a session and weekly meter, never a silent mid-session stop, grace mode in exam weeks; student plan USD 6–10 with an "until exams" SKU, priced against a tutor, not a notes site [A53][A54][SV wish 19].

We do *not* claim to be smarter than the frontier models (we run on them), to replace teachers [A5], or any learning multiplier [A7].

## 3. The product model: primary object, core loops (daily / weekly / exam-cycle), the user's first 10 minutes, the first week, the exam week

**Primary object: the Course** = board + syllabus code + version + level + exam session (e.g. "9609 Business, 2026–28, A Level, June 2027") [A2]. A Course owns a curriculum pack, a syllabus tree with a mastery state per point, an item bank (real past-paper questions where licensed, original board-style items by default [A33]), the student's Attempts, the FSRS queue, the planner and all conversations. "New chat" exists only *inside* a Course; a chat with no Course is an "Ask" and is never counted as study.

**Atomic record: the Attempt** `{item_id, syllabus_points[], response, transcript_confirmed, mark_per_AO, rung_reached, confidence, latency_to_first_token, seen_solution, timestamp}`. Every metric, mastery update, teacher view and the learning receipt are projections of Attempts; a feature that does not create or improve Attempts is not in the MVP.

**Three loops.**

*Daily (12–25 minutes, "Today").* Composition follows Rule D3: ≥60% due retrieval (cards, definitions, formula recall, mini-items), ≥25% interleaved mixed practice from the last three topics, ≤15% new material, never a blocked run of >5 same-type items after first exposure [A14]. Each Today contains exactly one *marked* exam-style item at real tariff (a 2–5-mark item on weekdays; an 8–12-mark item once or twice a week), because the marked Attempt is what moves the predicted grade. It ends with a 20-second predict-postdict on the marked item and a one-line "what changed" (mastery pips that moved). No streak, no confetti [A40].

*Weekly (Sunday, 10 minutes, "Readiness review").* The student sees: syllabus points that moved state; the calibration curve ("you said 'sure' 40 times; 26 were right"); the mark-yield list for next week (Section 7.3); one timed section scheduled; sleep-aware plan for the week [A60]. The weekly goal is learning events with rest days, never days-in-a-row [A40].

*Exam-cycle (September → results).* September: onboarding, cold-open diagnostics per topic, plan. October–November: first mock imported (photo or typed) → per-question post-mortem → weak-topic map. January: mock results framed as recoverable, with a plan not platitudes. Easter: past-paper blitz prioritised by marks-per-hour. Exam season: *between-papers mode* — next paper only, no re-litigating sat papers, 90-minute cap after 22:00 [SV#8][A60]. August: results, remark advice with real threshold context.

**The first 10 minutes** (target: a marked answer within 90 s of first typing [A43]).
- 0:00–0:40 — three screens: board → subjects (chips) → exam session/date. No account yet.
- 0:40–2:30 — "Try one": a 3-mark *Explain* item from the first subject appears with its mark tariff and command word highlighted. Composer shows the mode chip "Exam". The student types or photographs a handwritten answer; if photographed, the transcription appears for confirmation [A25].
- 2:30–3:30 — the marking card streams AO by AO: "AO1 1/1 — 'a benefit is…' ✓; AO2 1/2 — 'so the business can attract customers' is generic; to earn the second mark tie it to something true of *this* business." Beneath: "Real examiners disagree by ±1 on items like this" and a numbered citation to the mark scheme line [GM#B4(b)][A36].
- 3:30–6:00 — a 3-item cold-open retrieval probe on the same topic with confidence ratings (sure / fairly sure / guessing) [A14]; each answer gets task-level feedback, no praise.
- 6:00–8:00 — the Course home renders: syllabus tree with 4-state pips (all "Unseen" except the two just touched), the readiness ring, "Next exam in 243 days", and the first Today plan. One sentence explains the deal: "I'll mark like an examiner, I won't hand you answers before you've tried, and you can always ask for one."
- 8:00–10:00 — account creation to save the Course; parental consent flow if under 16 [A58]; optional import of a school timetable and existing Anki decks.

**The first week.** Day 1–2: cold-open diagnostics on the topics the school is currently teaching — productive failure before instruction (d=0.36) [A14]. Day 2–3: first help-ladder session; first "5 more like this" at fixed tariff [SV wish 8]. Day 3: first seen-solution retest fires [A11]. Day 4–6: first 8-mark *Analyse* item marked with the levels grid, showing that evaluation written into an Analyse item earned 0 [GM#B1.2]. Day 7: first Readiness review, first calibration curve, first pip reaching "Secure". By then the learner model holds ≥30 Attempts, enough for the mark-yield planner to drop cohort priors.

**The exam week.** The Course flips to between-papers mode at T−7 days per component: Today becomes a paper-specific plan (timed section → debrief → targeted retrieval on lost marks); the entry rung rises (no worked examples at Secure or above — expertise reversal [A10]); new material is locked unless overridden; the planner refuses more than 90 minutes after 22:00 and says why (month-before sleep ≈25% of grade variance [A60]); distress messages get the warm-brief-honest-back-to-plan script with crisis signposting [A60]. After each real paper: "Log how it felt", then nothing about that paper until results.

## 5. The unique bets (5–8): what we do that nobody does, why it works, how we would know it works

1. **The Examiner Twin with a published number.** One calibrated marker per paper (not per subject), standardised on ECRs and teacher double-marked scripts, seeded in production like a board, ensembled across two model families on essays, with a "challenge this mark" flow and a public calibration page [A22][A23][GM#A4.4]. *Why it works:* frontier models are already at the second-examiner line on real scripts; the missing ingredient is board-style QA, not intelligence [GM#0.1]. *How we know:* per-paper QWK ≥ human–human line on held-out gold; the "AI mark vs school mock mark" gap shrinks to human tolerance; challenge rate <3%, upheld <30%.
2. **Mark-Yield planning.** The planner ranks syllabus points by *expected marks recoverable per hour* — tariff exposure in the target papers × (1 − mastery) × forgetting × learnability — instead of by "weakest topic" (Section 7.3). *Why it works:* spacing and mastery learning both say effort should go where retrieval is about to fail on material that is heavily assessed; students cannot compute this from a spreadsheet [SV wish 7]. *How we know:* per hour studied, predicted-grade movement is higher in the mark-yield arm than in a weakest-topic arm (in-product RCT), and students' own prioritisation matches the planner more over time.
3. **Answer Anatomy — the derived layer.** Every item is linked to its structured marking points (M1…M5 with conjunctive conditions), levels grid per AO, indicative content, examiner-report criticisms, misconceptions and syllabus points [A32][CS#5]. *Why it works:* the founder's mechanisms file shows the value is the question → marking-point link, not the licensed text; it is also the legally defensible asset [A33]. *How we know:* mark-scheme adherence score in evals; the share of feedback lines that cite a specific marking point or descriptor ≥95%.
4. **Calibration as a first-class outcome.** Confidence on every gradable Attempt, predict–postdict–compare on mocks, a weekly calibration curve, overconfident-wrong items at ×0.5 interval with forced self-explanation [A14]. *Why it works:* monitoring accuracy is trainable (g≈0.57) and overconfident students skip preparatory work [LS#1.9]. *How we know:* Brier score falls per student over 8 weeks; the predicted-mark gap on mocks narrows; teachers report fewer "I thought I'd done well" surprises.
5. **The cognitive-debt guard with a visible dividend.** Six behavioural signals, three graduated responses (unaided opener → higher entry rung → brain-first mode), always paired with the student's own unaided-score delta [A16]. *Why it works:* order matters (think first, then AI preserved engagement in the MIT study), and self-confidence rather than confidence-in-AI predicts critical engagement [LS#2.1]. *How we know:* randomised on/off at user level; primary outcome 28-day delayed retention and mock marks; the guard must cost ≤2 pp 30-day retention.
6. **Exam-anchored memory.** FSRS-6 per atomic item with desired retention ramping 0.85 → 0.90 → 0.95, intervals capped at the exam date, Cepeda ratio as prior for new material, and — in the last three weeks — whole timed papers replacing card reviews as the interleaving mechanism [A52][A14]. *Why it works:* the optimal gap is a fraction of the retention interval, and the retention interval is *known* — the exam date [LS#1.3]. *How we know:* 7- and 28-day unaided recall on scheduled items ≥85%/≥80%; per-user FSRS log loss ≤0.35 once fitted.
7. **The Learning Receipt.** A signed, exportable process log (attempts, hints, rungs, drafts, timestamps) per assignment, plus a hard refusal to produce submittable coursework [A59]. *Why it works:* fear of false accusation is a top student pain and teachers endorse process visibility; nobody offers proof-of-work [SV wish 20][SV#5.3]. *How we know:* teacher endorsement rate in partner schools; receipts used in ≥1 in 5 integrity disputes reported by partner teachers; zero substantiated cases of the product writing coursework.
8. **Efficacy as product, not marketing.** In-product randomised experiments for every pedagogical policy (ladder entry rung, feedback timing, guard), and a pre-registered cluster RCT within two exam sessions, published whatever the result [A7][A61]. *Why it works:* the field has one platform RCT and no exam-board data; a credible number is a moat no lab will bother to build for one board [B Khanmigo]. *How we know:* the pre-registration is public before the first data point; effect estimates with confidence intervals are published per subject.

## 6. Information architecture and key screens: list every screen/surface with a 3–6 line description of what it shows and does (desktop and mobile)

IA: **Courses ▸ (Today · Syllabus · Practice · Papers · Review · Progress · Library)**, plus global **Search/⌘K**, **Settings/Memory**, and role surfaces (**Teacher**, **Parent**). Desktop is the three-pane workspace `280px 1fr 480px` (Library ▸ Chat ▸ Source/Scratchpad); tablet two panes; mobile one pane with bottom sheets and a full-screen Library route [A34]. Mode is a chip in the composer (Tutor / Exam / Explain / Quiz); the Course replaces any study-mode toggle [A35].

1. **Onboarding (3 screens + Try one).** Board → subjects → exam session; then a live 3-mark item marked within 90 s and a 3-item probe before any account [A43]. Mobile: identical, camera button prominent.
2. **Home / Today.** Shows work, not a greeting: Continue (last session), Today's plan (due · interleaved · new, with minutes), the one marked item, next exam countdown, and the weekly learning-event goal with rest days [A40][A43]. Mobile: this is the launch screen and the widget.
3. **Course home / Syllabus tree.** Official numbering with 4-state mastery pips (Unseen / Shaky / Secure / Exam-ready), decay shown as a fading pip with a "why" tooltip, mark-yield badges ("~4 marks recoverable"), and the readiness ring per component [A39]. Tap a point → its items, misconceptions, textbook pages.
4. **Tutor session (three-pane).** Centre chat at 68 ch, ≤120-word turns, rung indicator ("Hint 2 of 5"), intent chips, math keypad, camera; right pane shows the cited source page, scratchpad or worked-stepper; left pane the syllabus/session tree [A12][A36]. Mobile: bottom sheets.
5. **Practice runner.** One exam-style item at a time with tariff, command word and paper reference; confidence slider; effort gate enforced; "5 more like this" at fixed tariff; interleaved by default after first exposure [A14][A27].
6. **Marking view.** Streaming per-AO cards (first card <8 s, full <45 s): level chosen, quoted spans with line refs, what was *not* credited and why, next-mark advice in ER language, confidence band, "real examiners disagree by ±N", citation chips to scheme and ER, model provenance, and Challenge this mark [A19][A47]. Points-based items show ✓ × ECF ISW annotations inline on the transcript.
7. **Handwriting capture → confirm.** Crop, transcribe, side-by-side editable transcript with low-confidence tokens highlighted; the student confirms before marking; diagrams accept a structured description fallback [A25].
8. **Papers.** Past-paper browser by series/component (deep links to public PDFs; SSH material via teacher upload), the original-item bank, and "Start timed mock" [A33].
9. **Timed mock.** Full-screen, per-question pacing bars from minutes-per-mark, extra-time % and rest breaks, predict-before-start; on finish: postdict, marking, then the **Principal-Examiner debrief** [A26].
10. **Mock debrief.** Component mark vs last three series' thresholds (labelled historical), predicted grade range, marks-to-next-grade, lost-mark clusters with ER phrases, one-tap "turn into cards" [A24].
11. **Review (retrieval).** Two-button flashcards (Again / Got it), retrieval-first notes (question → reveal), self-explanation checkpoints; reading mode exists but is not counted as study [A39][LS#3.9]. Mobile: full-screen, two 56-px buttons.

## 7. Your lens in depth (the section where you go deepest — see lens brief)

### 7.1 Tutor behaviour spec

**Turn anatomy (tutoring modes; marking cards are exempt).** Every turn ≤120 words unless the student asked for depth, in this order: (1) task-level acknowledgement of what was right, specific and unadorned; (2) one diagnosis, named with the misconception label; (3) one move — a question *or* a hint *or* a micro-example; (4) hand back the pen with an explicit action [A12][LS#3.2]. The orchestrator rejects a draft that contains more than one question mark addressed to the student, solves beyond the current step, restates the whole problem, or contains person-praise, exclamation marks, emoji or "Great question" [A13][A17]. A rejected draft is regenerated once with the violation named; a second failure falls back to a template turn.

**Help ladder (Rules A1–A5).** Rung 1 metacognitive ("which concept family?"), rung 2 conceptual pointer (principle, no numbers), rung 3 procedural (next step only), rung 4 partial worked step with a blank, rung 5 full solution immediately followed by a near-transfer item the student must do unaided [A10]. One rung per turn; the ladder resets per item; the rung reached is stored on the Attempt. Entry rung from mastery: M<0.4 → rung 4 (novices gain d=0.505 from high assistance), M>0.7 → rung 1 and rung 4 is refused until two failed attempts (d=−0.428) [A10]. *Essay-subject variant:* rung 4 is a model *paragraph* one level above the student's current AO level with the evaluative sentence blanked, never a whole model essay; rung 5 for a 20-mark item is a plan (define → two context-linked chains → counter → weighted judgement) plus one exemplar paragraph, with the full model answer available only for past papers [LS#3.9]. Rule A5: "is it B?" gets "commit to a reason first" [LS#3.1].

**Effort gate.** No solution content on a gradable item before an attempt, a plan, or "don't know where to start" plus one sentence of what they do know [A9]. The gate is a state on the Attempt, not a refusal in prose; the composer shows it as a small "Your go first" chip with a 90-second time-box option ("give the hint 90 seconds; then full solution, no questions") [LS#3.10].

**Intent chips and "just give me the answer".** At task start: *Teach me* (ladder), *Check my steps* (student answer first, then confirmation with reasoning), *Just the answer* (allowed after one attempt) [A11]. The answer script is fixed: name the deal once ("Here it is — and one like it right after so it sticks"), deliver rung 5, label the item seen-solution (never counts toward Secure), schedule the retest at 24–72 h, show the receipt chip ("solution seen — retest Thursday"). Never lecture, never rate-limit, never deny withholding if asked [C5]. Homework-due-in-10-minutes is a legitimate intent: answer, then schedule an unaided redo tomorrow.

**Misconception diagnosis.** Every syllabus point carries ≥3 misconceptions mined from examiner reports with diagnostic distractors (9609: revenue vs profit, cash flow vs profit, naming the business as "application", evaluation written into Analyse items, inverted ratios, missing ×100) [A15][GM#B1.6]. On a wrong answer the tutor asks for the reasoning trace ("talk me through how you got 42"), classifies slip / misconception / missing prerequisite, and routes: slip → quick fix and move on; misconception → contrast case (two items differing in one feature; "which rule applies and why?"); prerequisite → detour to the prerequisite node, which is then scheduled [LS#3.3]. The classification is stored and feeds the misconception prior for the student.

**Fading.** Per-skill state machine: worked example → completion (last step blank) → completion (first step given) → full item with hints → full item, no hints, timed; advance after two consecutive correct, regress after two consecutive errors [LS#3.4]. A self-explanation checkpoint gates every worked example ("why divide by contribution per unit here?") (g=0.55). The same hint type is not offered twice for a skill within a week; the third time the student is asked to generate the hint.

**No-sycophancy rules.** Banned: person-praise, unearned praise, agreement with an incorrect claim, softening a mark. Required: feedback = task → process → self-regulation, opening with the highest-yield lost mark [A13]. Weekly praise audit on 1% of turns (2% in essay subjects) with a praise-vs-contribution rubric; target <5% [LS#3.6 E4]. The "disagreeable teacher with standards" is the voice model [SV wish 18].

**Enforcement and evals.** Pedagogy lives in cached system instructions plus the vetting orchestrator; every prompt or model change must pass 30-turn simulated-student conversations including adversarial "just tell me" personas, scored on SafeTutors dimensions, with answer-leak <5%, multi-turn failure rate reported, and subject-specific profiles (leak controls in quantitative subjects, sycophancy controls in essay subjects) [A17][LS#3.12]. The tutor pre-solves every item with a strong model and SymPy and holds the key [A18][A51].

### 7.2 The exam engine

**Marking output (per part).** `total → per-AO split → per-AO level with quoted spans and line refs → uncredited attempts with reason → next-mark advice in ER language → confidence band → sources → provenance`. A credited point without a quote is a bug that fails the eval [GM#B4(b)]. Confidence is per AO; "show teacher" flags ambiguous AO4 judgements.

**Two engines, board procedures.** *Points engine:* M/A/B/DM/DB types, conjunctive conditions with all-required flags, accept/reject/ignore lists, ECF computed from the candidate's own prior values within the scheme's scope, ISW, CAO, units and sig-fig penalties once per paper, SymPy recompute of every numeric step, "//" alternatives credited once [A20]. *Levels engine:* each AO levelled independently from the full grid with all levels defined (accuracy falls as levels are omitted), best-fit within an AO only, "developed" operationalised as counted causal links (≥2) not words, application credited only for facts true of *this* business (cover-the-name test), evaluation-in-Analyse detected and scored 0 with the reason [A19][GM#Impl 2,6]. Per-board procedure prompts: Cambridge best-fit, AQA ladder-up, OCR top-down, Pearson bullet-level evidence [A19].

**Command-word compliance.** Before marking, the command word is matched to its AO ceiling (Define/State → AO1; Explain/Calculate → AO1–2; Analyse → AO1–3; Discuss/Evaluate/Assess/Recommend → AO1–4); a mismatch caps the level and produces one coaching line [A21][GM#B1.4].

**Calibration, generosity, gates.** Context always includes the grid, generic marking principles, the command-word definition, indicative content and 3–5 ECR exemplars with commentary; marker instructions (never the official grid) are refined Reflect-and-Revise style against ~200 human-marked scripts per question family until QWK plateaus, then versioned [GM#Impl 3,4]. Determinism: temperature 0, structured JSON, n=3 self-consistency with median AO marks for ≥12-mark items, negative exemplars and an examiner persona against LLM leniency, dual-family ensemble with escalation to a third model then a human when disagreement exceeds one level [A23]. Gates are relative to the human line on the same gold items: points exact ≥97% / ±1 ≥99.5%; 8–12-mark levels QWK ≥ human–human line with floor 0.75; 20-mark essays QWK ≥ human–human line with floor 0.70, within ±2 ≥85%, AO4 level agreement ≥80%, rationale validity ≥95%; signed error within ±0.3/10; run-to-run identical total ≥95% and identical level 100%; no fairness bucket (region, L1, handwritten, length) lagging by >0.05 [A22][GM#A4.2–A4.3]. Production seeding 2–5%, tolerance breach pauses the question family and falls back to a second model with a "provisional" badge. Marks and predicted grades are hidden below gate — feedback still shows [A22].

**Predicted grade.** Component raw → last-three-series thresholds → PUM → option grade; displayed as a range with the series used, the spread and "marks to next grade" per component; A* only at option level; labelled "practice estimate, not a board mark"; nothing flows into a school-recorded grade without teacher confirmation [A24][C11].

**Question generation.** Constrained by tariff, the paper's AO split, command word and insert conventions; emits a board-format scheme at the same time; passes a style-similarity check and an independent-model solvability check; never reproduces real paper text; "5 more like this" holds difficulty fixed by tariff and cognitive-demand tag [A27][SV wish 8].

**Timed mocks and debrief.** Minutes-per-mark from paper metadata (9702 P5 = 2.5 min/mark), extra-time percentages, rest breaks that stop the clock, predict-before and postdict-after; the debrief is generated only from the marked Attempts and the ER corpus: lost-mark clusters, ER phrases ("candidates who scored well…"), threshold context, and three scheduled retrieval tasks [A26][GM#Impl 12,13].

**Handwriting.** Photo → VLM transcription → editable confirmation with low-confidence tokens highlighted → mark from transcript *and* image; illegible tokens flagged never guessed; mechanism and diagram items default to "provisional — teacher confirm" until a ≥300-item diagram gold set exists [A25][GM#Impl 8].

### 7.3 The learner model

**Mastery per syllabus point** is a continuous estimate M_s ∈ [0,1] surfaced as four states: *Unseen* (no Attempt); *Shaky* (attempted; or Secure with FSRS retrievability <0.7); *Secure* (two unaided correct across ≥2 days at the point's tariff, no seen-solution credit); *Exam-ready* (Secure plus one correct under timed conditions at full tariff with calibrated confidence). M_s is the retrievability-weighted mean over the point's items of a correctness posterior, penalised by rung depth (a rung-5 correct contributes 0). Decay is FSRS-driven, so pips fade rather than lie [A39][A52]. Both the state and the estimate carry a "why" tooltip.

**Memory scheduling.** FSRS-6 per atomic item (definition, formula, worked skill, evaluation point, diagram rule); desired retention 0.85 (>8 weeks), 0.90 (3–8), 0.95 (<3); intervals capped at the exam date; Cepeda prior (first review at 10–20% of time-to-exam) for new material inside 8 weeks; shrinkage fit from ~400 reviews, full parameters from ~1,000 [A52][C8]. Overconfident-wrong items get ×0.5 interval and mandatory self-explanation [A14].

**Mark-yield priority.** For each syllabus point s in the target component set: `yield_s = tariff_exposure_s × (1 − M_s) × (1 − R_s(t_exam)) × learnability_s / est_hours_s`, where tariff_exposure is marks attributable to s across the last six series (from the tagged item bank), R_s is FSRS retrievability projected to the exam, learnability decays with attempts already spent on s, and est_hours comes from cohort medians until the student has data. The planner fills the ≤15% "new" and ≥25% "interleaved" slots from the top of this list; the ≥60% "due" slot is FSRS-driven [A14].

**Calibration.** Brier score from confidence ratings, per topic and overall; predict–postdict–compare on every mock; the tutor comments on the gap, not the score [A14].

**Cognitive-debt guard.** Signals over a session and a rolling 14 days: solution-request ratio, attempt latency <8 s, copy-paste of tutor text, re-explanation without attempt, widening confidence–accuracy gap, output-creation vs practice ratio. Responses: L1 next session opens with three unaided items on yesterday's seen-solution items; L2 entry rung +1 for 48 h; L3 brain-first mode (tutor read-only until an attempt) — always paired with the student's unaided-score delta [A16][LS#3.11].

### 7.4 Teachers and parents

Teachers are *calibrators and endorsers* before they are customers. MVP: class code, syllabus-locked assignments with an allowed-help level, attempts-and-hints view, AI-mark audit with one-click override flowing into the gold set, mock import, and a calibration-marking queue (teachers double-mark ~20 scripts per question family, credited) [A55][GM#A4.1]. Teachers see aggregate ladder depth and seen-solution counts, never per-request policing [C5]. Parents get a fortnightly aggregate digest, no transcripts, and the explicit "does not write homework" promise [A55]. Neither surface ever says "replaces the teacher" [A5].

### 7.5 Efficacy measurement plan

*Layer 1 — continuous in-product experiments* (user-level randomisation, pre-specified): guard on/off; entry-rung policy; immediate vs brief-delay conceptual feedback (E3); mark-yield vs weakest-topic planning. Primary outcomes: 7/28-day unaided retention, transfer items, mock component marks; guard-rail: 30-day retention [A16][A61].
*Layer 2 — observational, first exam session (June 2027 results):* predicted-vs-actual grade error per paper; grade movement relative to teacher-predicted grades with propensity matching against non-users in partner schools; reported as associational, never causal.
*Layer 3 — pre-registered cluster RCT (October 2027 – June 2028 series)*: 9609/0450 in ~40 classes across partner schools, randomised at class level, control = school practice plus a frontier chat study mode; primary outcome = component raw mark on a school-administered mock marked blind by external examiners; secondary = actual board grades vs predicted, 28-day retention, calibration; MDE 0.25 SD; analysis plan and stopping rules public before the first data point; results published whatever they show [A7][A61].
*Marking calibration* is published per paper monthly (QWK, level agreement, signed error, fairness buckets). Marketing may cite only numbers produced by these layers.

## 8. What we deliberately do NOT do (and why), and where you disagree with INDEX decisions (say so explicitly with reasons)

**Not doing.** No answer business (Chegg's collapse) [A4]; no "replaces teachers" messaging [A5]; no daily streaks, loss animations or shame counters [A40]; no submittable coursework [A59]; no marks or predicted grades below gate or without a scheme in context [A18][A22]; no efficacy multiplier in marketing [A7]; no image/video generation, agents or ads [FB#6]; no full-text re-hosting of board material before licences — deep links and short attributed extracts only [A33][GL#0]; no Socratic-refusal theatre — "Just the answer" is always available, labelled [A11]; no engagement optimisation [A61]; no English literature or Psychology marking in MVP (lowest human reliability) [GM#B4(c)].

**Disagreements with INDEX.**
1. **[A22] absolute QWK gates.** The 0.80 (8–12 marks) and 0.75 (20–30 marks) floors ignore that human–human reliability in essay subjects is 0.56–0.74 [GM#0.2]; a 20-mark 9609 essay may never reach 0.75 against a single human mark and does not need to. The gate should be *"≥ the human–human line on the same gold items"* with floors 0.75 / 0.70, plus the run-to-run and rationale-validity conditions [GM#A4.3]. The 0.80 unlock for predicted grades [C4] should likewise be "≥ human line".
2. **[A14] the 60/25/15 mix as a constant.** In the last three weeks whole timed papers should *replace* card reviews as the interleaving mechanism; a student doing 60% flashcards in exam week is mis-served. The mix should be phase-dependent (Section 5 bet 6).
3. **[A55] full teacher mode from day one.** The MVP needs teachers as *calibrators* (double-marking, overrides, class code, assignments); the full console and LMS connectors are v1 [E16]. Console breadth in the MVP delays the calibration data that gates everything else.
4. **[A61] north-star list.** Six co-equal metrics is no north-star. For this lens the single north-star is *component-mark gain on unaided, blind-marked mocks per hour studied*, with predicted-vs-actual error as the trust metric and delayed retention, transfer and Brier as diagnostics.
5. **[A10] entry rung by mastery alone.** Entry should also depend on item type: for levels-based essay items a rung-4 "worked example" must be a paragraph at one level above the student, never a full model answer, otherwise the ladder itself becomes the answer business.
6. **[A44] routing detail is secondary.** Which model tutors matters less than whether the orchestrator vets the turn; fund evals and calibration scripts [E6] before any RL-tuned tutoring layer [E12].

## 10. Metrics of success (north-star, guard-rails, per-feature) and the biggest risks with mitigations

**North-star:** component-mark gain on unaided, blind-marked mocks per hour studied (in-product) and, once available, actual grade vs teacher-predicted grade for active users [A61]. **Trust metric:** predicted-vs-actual grade error (target: within one grade for ≥80% of active users by the second session). **Diagnostics:** 7/28-day unaided retention (≥85%/≥80% on scheduled items), transfer score on unseen items, mastery velocity (Secure points per study hour), Brier score trend, help-ladder depth trend. **Guard-rails:** answer-leak rate <5%; praise rate <5%; multi-turn pedagogy failure rate reported per release; marking QWK/level agreement per paper vs gate; run-to-run ≤1 mark; 30-day retention; cost per student-month (median ≤$2.6, heavy ≤$13) [A57]; sessions after 22:00 in exam week; DAU and minutes reported, never targeted.

## 11. Names: 3 candidate product names with rationale (avoid exam-board marks)

All three avoid board marks (Cambridge, IGCSE, GCSE, SAT, AP) and need UKIPO/EUIPO/USPTO and domain checks before use [E10].

1. **Bestfit** — from the Cambridge marking instruction "make a judgement about which level statement is the best fit" [GM#B1.2]. Insider signal to teachers and examiners, plain meaning to students ("the best fit for your exam"); risk: descriptive, weak as a mark — pair with a distinctive wordmark.
2. **Kalibre** — calibration is the product's spine (calibrated marker, calibrated self-knowledge) and *calibre* means quality; coined spelling is ownable and pronounceable in English, Russian and Kazakh; risk: near "Calibra/Calibre" marks in other classes.
3. **Retrieva** — retrieval practice is the highest-utility technique the product is built on (g=0.61) [B]; coined, feminine Latinate ending travels across markets, short domain variants likely available; risk: sounds like a database product to engineers, which the design and voice must offset.
