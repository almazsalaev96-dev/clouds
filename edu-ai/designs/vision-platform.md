# Vision — Platform and company first — the system, content engine, economics and distribution that make this the best company

*Lens: founder-CTO/COO, 6 September 2026. Citations: INDEX decisions [A12], table rows [B-row], contradictions [C3], founder questions [E4]; corpus-sample [CS]; gap-marking-and-cambridge [GM]; gap-legal-and-incumbents [GL]. Written to be built.*

## 1. One-paragraph thesis (what this product is, in your lens's terms)

This is an **exam-outcome company built as a data platform**, with a chat interface on top. The durable asset is not the model or the UI but a **curriculum pack**: one board-agnostic schema that holds a syllabus (versioned, numbered), its papers and questions, its mark schemes decomposed into structured marking points and levels grids, examiner-report insights indexed to questions, textbook sections, a syllabus graph with cross-board equivalence edges, and per-source trust ranks [A29][A31][A32]. Around each pack we run four engines that the labs will not build per board: an examiner-calibrated marker with a published agreement number per paper [A3][A22], a learner graph keyed to syllabus points with FSRS scheduling [A48][A52], a curriculum compiler that turns a new board's PDFs into a pack in weeks [A29], and a multi-tenant workflow (student, parent, teacher, school, tutor) that schools can endorse [A55]. Table-stakes chat is matched and treated as cost of entry [A1]. Economics: median student ≈$1.6–2.6/month of model spend against a $6–10 price point, with quotas on Opus-class marks carrying the heavy tail [A53][A57]. Distribution: free teacher mode, creators, exam-calendar seasonality, school seats [A55][A56]. Moats: calibration data, tagged item banks with statistics, the learner graph, and trust — none of which a lab feature toggle can copy [A8].

## 2. Positioning: the honest answer to "why would a student use this instead of ChatGPT / Gemini / Claude (with their free study modes) + Save My Exams?" — 8–12 defensible differentiators, each tied to evidence [cite]

The honest baseline is strong: free study modes in all three labs plus Save My Exams' (SME) topic questions and human model answers [A1][GL §B5]; bundling the two is not worth paying for. These differentiators survive that test, in order of defensibility.

1. **A marker calibrated against real examiner marks, with the number published per paper.** Students' #1 pain is lenient, inconsistent AI marking ("ChatGPT predicts me like 16 marks and in reality I'll get 3") and no competitor publishes agreement statistics [A3][GL §B3 item 2]. 2026 evidence on 32,534 double-marked real GCSE scripts shows frontier models at or above second-examiner consistency in maths/science and near it in essays [GM §0 item 1]; the human line is QWK ≈0.84 in maths and only 0.52–0.74 P(definitive grade) in essay subjects [GM §0 item 2]. We ship marking per paper only above tiered gates (points exact ≥97%; 8–12-mark QWK ≥0.80; 20–30-mark QWK ≥0.75) and hide it below [A22]. Defensible because it needs paired (script, examiner mark) data per paper that accumulates with use [BM §3.1].
2. **Marking per Assessment Objective with quoted evidence spans, following the board's own procedure.** Cambridge best-fit per AO grid, "developed analysis = two or more links in the chain", the cover-the-business-name test for AO2 [A19][GM §B1.2]. ChatGPT marks holistically from imagination; SME's Smart Mark does short answers only [GL §B5]. A mark with no quoted span is a bug.
3. **Examiner-report voice.** Every "next mark" tip is phrased and cited the way Principal Examiners phrase it ("evaluation sections very limited"; "repeating the business name will not count as application") [GM §B1.6][GL §B4 item 1]. Nobody surfaces ERs in an AI loop.
4. **The Course, not "New chat", is the home object**: board + syllabus code + level + session, with mode as a visible chip [A2][A35]. No lab maps answers to a syllabus code, AO or command word; Study Mode cannot even run inside Projects [A2].
5. **The learner graph remembers what you know, not what you said.** Mastery per syllabus point with decay, misconception log mined from ERs, seen-solution items scheduled for 24–72 h retest, FSRS-6 with retention ramping toward the exam date [A14][A15][A48][A52]. No chat product schedules anything; Reddit users export to Anki by hand [FB §7 item 4].
6. **Predicted grade with provenance**: raw → component threshold → PUM → option grade, shown as a range across recent series with "marks to next grade" [A24]. Up Learn sells a guarantee; we sell the auditable dashboard behind it [EC §6 item 4].
7. **Effort-gated pedagogy that is measured, not toggled.** Unguarded GPT raised practice +48% but lowered exam scores; a guard-railed tutor raised practice +127% with no harm [A9]. Off-the-shelf models leak the answer in the opening turn >96% of the time [A17]. Our answer-leak rate is a CI gate (<5%) [A61], and "just the answer" is honoured after one attempt, labelled, and retested — never refused [A11].
8. **A compliant content model schools can recommend.** Cambridge refuses electronic publication of mark schemes and examiner reports; every CAIE "papers + AI" app runs on tolerance [GL §0][GL §A2]. We ship three provenance classes (OWN / LICENSED / USER), deep links, transient bring-your-own-paper processing and original items as the default bank [A33][GL impl. 1–2]. Being the compliant one is a moat, not a constraint.
9. **Machine-verified numerics and no invented quotes.** Every numeric final passes SymPy before display; quotes come only from loaded set texts [A51] — hallucinated chemistry and quotes are top-4 pains [SV §2.1].
10. **Teen-safe, teacher-visible, parent-informed by design.** Age assurance, Study/Quiet Hours, no companion behaviour, no ads, Article 50 disclosure, an Annex III technical file before 2 Dec 2027 [A58]; teacher mode free from day one; parents get a fortnightly digest without transcripts [A55]. Gemini's best learning surfaces and Claude are 18+; only ChatGPT has a real teen product, US-first [FB §7 item 10].
11. **Limits published in plain numbers with a meter, never cut silently.** Limit opacity is the labs' worst complaint (Claude megathread 799↑/6,270 comments) and SME's free tier is five notes a month [A54][GL §B3 item 1]; ours stays useful, with grace mode in exam weeks.
12. **Every pixel calmer and faster.** Three-pane workspace, TTFT p50 <800 ms, citation to the exact page in <400 ms, no streaks or confetti [A34][A36][A38][A40]. Necessary, not sufficient — but students notice within 30 seconds [EC §6 item 10].

What we do **not** claim: that we replace past papers or teachers [A5], that AI marks are official, that we are endorsed by Cambridge, or any efficacy multiplier that is not pre-registered [A7].

## 3. The product model: primary object, core loops (daily / weekly / exam-cycle), the user's first 10 minutes, the first week, the exam week

**Primary object: the Course.** `Course = {board, syllabus_code, syllabus_version, level, exam_session, target_grade, papers[]}` — e.g. "Cambridge A Level Business 9609 (2026–28) · June 2027 · target A". Everything else (conversations, attempts, cards, plan, predicted grade, teacher class) hangs off a Course [A2]. A student typically has 3–4 Courses; a teacher's class binds to one Course; a parent sees Courses, not chats. Inside a Course the four modes are chips in the composer — Tutor / Exam / Explain / Quiz — never a settings toggle [A35].

**Secondary objects:** the Attempt (answer + mark card + evidence spans + policy version + teacher override), the Item (any atomic retrievable), the Plan (exam-date spine), and the Pack (§7).

**Daily loop (10–25 minutes).** The Today screen is generated by the scheduler, not the user [EC §8 item 9]: ≥60% due reviews, ≥25% interleaved practice, ≤15% new material [A14]. A typical day: three due retrieval items with confidence ratings → one 8- or 12-mark question in Exam mode → marked per AO in <45 s → the lost mark becomes a scheduled item → a ≤120-word tutor turn on the specific gap [A12]. No streak counter; the day ends with "done for today" and a one-line reason [A40].

**Weekly loop.** A weekly learning-event goal with rest days [A40]; the planner re-plans against marked performance and the exam spine [EC §8 item 12]; the predicted-grade range updates with "the three things that move you up a grade" [A24]; the parent digest goes out fortnightly, the teacher sees aggregate ladder depth and AO weaknesses per class [A55][C5]. Sunday: a 15-minute mixed retrieval probe across all Courses.

**Exam-cycle loop (September → mocks → Easter → exam → results).** September: diagnostic probe per syllabus section seeds mastery (3–5 items per topic, confidence-rated) [A14]. Mocks (November/January): timed Paper mode with minutes-per-mark pacing and an examiner-style debrief; predict–postdict–compare on every mock [A26][A14]. Easter: retention target rises to 0.90 at 3–8 weeks out, 0.95 inside 3 weeks; intervals cap at the paper date [A52]. Exam fortnight: grace mode on free-tier quotas [A54], the sleep-aware planner refuses late cramming, distress messages are handled warmly and briefly [A60]. Results day: consented actual grade imported → the predicted-vs-actual delta feeds calibration and the public efficacy record [A61]; one-click roll-over to the next qualification or session [BM implications 2].

**First 10 minutes (no account yet).** Screen 1: pick board → subjects → exam date (≤3 screens) [A43]. Screen 2: one real 8-mark-style question from the chosen syllabus, typed or photographed. Within 90 s the student sees a per-AO mark card with quoted evidence and one examiner-report line [A43]. Only then: create an account (age band, consent path). Minute 6–10: a 5-item retrieval probe seeds mastery pips for the topic just attempted; the Today screen appears with tomorrow's three items and the exam spine. The student has felt the marker, the citation chip and the calm before being asked for anything.

**First week.** Day 1: diagnostic probe for one syllabus section; Course memory shows what it stored, editable. Day 2–3: first Tutor session with the effort gate and a labelled "just the answer" (followed by a near-transfer item) [A11]. Day 4: first photo-of-handwriting mark with transcript confirmation [A25]. Day 5: first 20-mark essay marked on Opus-class with an AO4 coaching line. Day 7: weekly summary, predicted-grade range appears (provisional, wide) once ≥3 marked attempts per paper exist; the student is invited to add a class code or a parent.

**Exam week.** The Course collapses to three surfaces: the timetable, the due queue (capped, high-yield, no new material) and Paper mode. At most one factual notification per day ("Paper 2 tomorrow 09:00 — 1h30 — 60 marks; weakest AO: AO4"); no countdown panic, no motivational copy. After each real paper, a brief check-in and the planner rebalances toward the remaining components.

## 5. The unique bets (5–8): what we do that nobody does, why it works, how we would know it works

1. **The published calibration number.** *What:* for every supported paper we display "AI mark agrees with examiner marks: QWK 0.81 (n=312 scripts, June 2026 series)" and hide marking below gate. *Why it works:* it turns the category's biggest trust failure (Medly's "fundamentally broken" marking; three AIs giving 4/6, 5/6, 6/6) into an auditable claim nobody else makes [A22][GL §B3 item 2], and Ofqual's reliability numbers make "as consistent as a second examiner" credible [GM §0 item 2]. *How we'd know:* weekly QWK per paper per model version on a held-out gold set; run-to-run ≤1 mark; the number is the same in marketing and in the product; conversion lift on the mark-card surface.
2. **Marking points and levels grids as data, not prose.** *What:* the mark-scheme record type (M1…M5 with conjunctive conditions, M/A/B/DM/FT/CAO/ISW, `evidence_rule` per level band, `best_fit` flag) is a first-class table, editable by the founder and teachers via paste → structured [CS §5 item 3][GM §B4a]. *Why:* the founder's most valuable file is exactly this derived layer; it is what turns an LLM into an examiner and what a lab will never curate per board [CS §3][BM §3.1]. *How we'd know:* per-marking-point precision/recall ≥0.9; teacher edits per week; time-to-structured-record <2 minutes per question part.
3. **The curriculum compiler.** *What:* an internal pipeline plus review UI that ingests syllabus + papers + schemes + ERs + thresholds for a new board and emits a gated pack in <4 weeks, no code [A29][BM impl. 18]. *Why:* CAIE alone is 10,000 schools in 160 countries, but the founder's mission is every curriculum; packs-as-data is the only way the org scales sub-linearly with boards. *How we'd know:* wall-clock from PDFs to gate per pack, trending down; share of pack content that passed human QA on first pass; number of live packs per content engineer.
4. **The cross-board syllabus graph.** *What:* `equivalent_to` edges (CAIE 9609 "price elasticity" ≈ Edexcel Theme 1.2.3 ≈ IB Econ 2.x) so one explanation bank, misconception list and item pool serve many curricula with terminology mapping [AR §2.5]. *Why:* it makes the second board 3× cheaper than the first and lets a student move IGCSE → A Level → IB → university in one product [EC §6 bonus]. *How we'd know:* percentage of a new pack's topics covered by mapped existing content at compile time (target >60% for a sibling board).
5. **The three-provenance content model as a sales weapon.** *What:* OWN / LICENSED / USER classes enforced at the retrieval layer; no "scraped" class exists in production; deep links to official PDFs; a corpus provenance ledger with licence basis and expiry; published takedown SLA [GL impl. 1][GL §A5]. *Why:* schools cannot officially recommend PapaCambridge-style tools; the compliant player wins procurement and the Cambridge licensing conversation [GL §B4 item 4]. *How we'd know:* school pilots won where compliance was the stated reason; zero takedowns; licensing deals signed.
6. **Teacher mode free, forever, from day one.** *What:* class code, syllabus-locked assignments, attempt/hint visibility, audit-and-override of AI marks, allowed-help level — no charge to teachers [A55]. *Why:* Khanmigo reached 795 districts at ~$10/student/yr because teachers got it free [B Khanmigo]; teacher overrides are also our cheapest calibration labour [AR impl. 23]. *How we'd know:* activation per licensed school >60%; overrides reduce model–examiner disagreement quarter over quarter; teacher NPS.
7. **The learner graph as the switching cost we are proud of.** *What:* mastery per syllabus point, misconceptions, seen-solution items, calibration (Brier) history, predicted-vs-actual — exportable as CSV/PDF and importable from Anki/Quizlet [BM impl. 8]. *Why:* memory alone is cheap to copy; progress linked to a syllabus map and predicted grades is not; making it exportable is what earns the trust to keep it [BM §3.1]. *How we'd know:* W12 retention for students with ≥200 scheduled items vs without; export-then-churn rate (should be low).
8. **Honest economics shown to the user.** *What:* a usage meter, plain-number limits, grace mode in exam weeks, and cost-aware routing that never degrades silently [A54][A57]. *Why:* limit opacity is the labs' worst complaint and SME's paywall is the reason a "free alternative" SEO industry exists [GL §B3 item 1]. *How we'd know:* support tickets about limits per 1,000 MAU; free→paid conversion in May/June vs a control cohort without grace mode.

## 7. Your lens in depth (the section where you go deepest — see lens brief)

### 7.1 The curriculum pack — one schema for every board

A pack is data; a new board is ingestion, never code [A29]. Postgres tables (all with `org_id NULL` for the global corpus, RLS for tenants) [A49]:

- `boards(id, name, country, marking_paradigm ∈ {expert-marked, machine-scored, mixed}, level_procedure ∈ {best_fit, ladder_up, top_down}, annotation_vocab jsonb)`.
- `syllabi(id, board_id, code "9609", version_years [2026,2028], level, subject, command_words jsonb (verbatim table + AO ceiling), grade_scale, source_url, public bool)`; `syllabus_nodes(id, syllabus_id, parent_id, path "1.2.3", title, objective_text, misconceptions[] , diagnostic_distractors[])` [A15].
- `papers(id, syllabus_id, number, duration_min, marks, ao_weights jsonb {AO1:35,AO2:30,...}, question_types[], allowed_materials)` — 9609 P1 35/30/20/15, P4 15/10/40/35 [GM §B1.1]; `thresholds(paper_id, series, grade, min_raw)` and `option_thresholds(syllabus_id, series, option, grade, pum)` [A24].
- `questions(id, paper_id, series "M/J/2025", variant 41, part "1(b)(ii)", marks, command_word, ao_ceiling, stimulus_ref, text_status ∈ {own, licensed, ref_only})` — for Cambridge, `ref_only` by default: we store the canonical ID and metadata, not the text [GL §A5].
- `mark_scheme_items(id, question_id, scheme_type ∈ {points, levels, mixed}, marking_points jsonb [{id:"M1", type:M|A|B|DM|DB, credit_text, conjunctive[], all_required, depends_on, ft_allowed, cao, isw, accept[], reject[], ao}], levels jsonb [{ao, max, bands:[{level, range, descriptor, evidence_rule}]}], best_fit, ecf_policy, indicative_content[], generic_principles_ref, wording_status ∈ {own_paraphrase, licensed_verbatim})` [GM §B4a][CS §5].
- `examiner_insights(id, question_id | syllabus_node_id, series, pattern ∈ {scored_well, weaker_responses, common_error}, text (our paraphrase), source_citation, heuristic jsonb)` — machine-checkable heuristics ("evaluation present in Analyse item → 0 AO4 marks available") plus a student-facing tip [ES impl. 25].
- `textbook_sections(id, document_id, path, page_start, page_end, licence_id)`; `exemplars(id, question_id, source ∈ {ECR, teacher_double_marked, synthetic_human_marked}, marks jsonb per AO, commentary, handwritten, blob)` [GM §A4.1].
- `links(src, dst, kind ∈ {assesses, answered_by, commented_on, explained_in, prerequisite_of, equivalent_to}, confidence, provenance)` — the syllabus graph; recursive CTEs, no graph DB [A32][AR §2.5].
- `documents.trust_rank`: syllabus 1 > endorsed textbook 2 > mark scheme 3 > examiner report 4 > revision notes 5 > user upload 6; retrieval filters by `syllabus_code` and `version` before similarity and ranks by trust on ties [A31].
- `items(id, kind, fsrs_params)` with `item_stats(n, p_correct, discrimination, mean_latency)` — the statistics layer that becomes IRT for machine-scored exams [A28].

Pack readiness is a computed state: `draft → ingested → mapped (≥95% of questions linked to scheme items) → gold-seeded (≥3 exemplars per question family) → gated (marking gates per paper) → live`. Predicted grades unlock only at `gated` with ≥3 series of thresholds loaded.

### 7.2 The curriculum compiler — a new board in weeks

Target by end of Year 1: syllabus PDFs → live pack in <4 weeks for a sibling board, <8 for a new paradigm [BM impl. 18]. Stages, each with a human review UI and a first-pass-acceptance metric:

1. **Acquire and ledger** — official URLs (public), teacher-uploaded SSH material (tenant-scoped), licensed files; every document gets `licence_basis ∈ {own, licensed, user, public_with_permission}`, `expiry`, `source_url`, sha256 [GL §A5]. There is no `scraped` class.
2. **Parse (two passes)** — Docling structural parse (reading order, tables, formulas, figure crops, page provenance) + VLM page audit on Gemini 3.8 Flash batch, escalated to 3.1 Pro/Opus 5 for flagged pages; per-page confidence; disagreements queue for humans [A30]. Per-source cleaning rules handle the founder's defects: bullet glyphs extracted as "Game", ×3–×6 duplicated blocks (fuzzy-hash dedupe), flattened superscripts and units, merged table headers, informal paper IDs ("S15 v21" → 9701/21/M/J/15) [CS §2]. Old-scan maths is the weakest parser category (29–84), so every equation page takes the VLM pass [B OCR row].
3. **Type and segment** — syllabus / QP / MS / ER / thresholds / textbook / notes; QP → question → part → marks; MS → marking points or level bands; ER → question-indexed commentary [AR §2.1].
4. **Structure the scheme** — LLM proposes `marking_points`/`levels` JSON in our own words; a content editor confirms in the paste → structured UI (<2 min per part). Conjunctive conditions ("lone pair AND curly arrow from lone pair to carbonyl C") are split into checkable atoms [CS §2 item 9].
5. **Map to the syllabus** — note numbering is not official numbering; a per-source mapping table with review [CS §5 item 4]; `equivalent_to` edges proposed against existing packs.
6. **Mine the examiner reports** — "candidates who scored well…/weaker responses…" pairs → `examiner_insights` with heuristics; misconception lists per syllabus node [A15][GM §B1.6].
7. **Seed gold** — ECRs, specimen answers, teacher double-marked scripts (~300 per paper, budget in [E6]); stratify by tariff, command word, handwritten/typed, length [GM §A4.1].
8. **Standardise and gate** — the marker must mark the seed set within tolerance per question family before the paper goes live; Reflect-and-Revise on ~200 held-out human-marked scripts refines marker instructions (not the official grid) until QWK plateaus [GM §A4.4][GM §0 item 4].
9. **Publish** — pack version pinned per Course; syllabus revisions (9608 → 9618; 9609 2023–25 → 2026–28) create a new version, never overwrite [CS §1].

Steps 4 and 7 are human-bound; de-risk with an editing UI faster than prose, ex-examiners paid per structured record, and reuse of the 9609 grid engine for 0450/9708 [GM §B2].

### 7.4 Content licensing posture and the conversations to start now

Posture: OWN + USER + deep links at launch; short paraphrased, cited extracts with caps and an audit log; licence before hosting or quoting Cambridge mark schemes or textbooks at scale [A33][GL §0]. Cambridge explicitly refuses electronic publication of questions, mark schemes and examiner reports; UK law offers no commercial TDM exception (March 2026 report); *Thomson Reuters v. Ross* is the adverse analogue for a RAG product that displays editorial content [GL §A4]. Therefore: no Cambridge mark-scheme viewer; the Paper Companion anchors feedback to question numbers on the student's own copy; ER insights are our paraphrase with series citation; the practice bank is original items with a near-duplicate check against the reference bank [GL impl. 2–5].

Conversations, in priority order, all started in month 1 with counsel: (1) **Cambridge University Press & Assessment** — one counterparty owns the board, the endorsed coursebooks and an opt-in AI-licensing programme on the academic side; ask for retrieval-grounding rights to mark schemes/ERs (not display), endorsed-textbook RAG, and descriptive non-endorsement use of the name [GL §A3][E2]; (2) **CLA Generative AI licence** — which school publishers (Hodder/OUP/Collins) are in repertoire and whether RAG display by a commercial SaaS is covered; (3) **Pearson Edexcel and AQA** ahead of the UK expansion (public windows of 3–5 years, centre-only permissions); (4) **College Board** permission request for systematic FRQ reuse (lower risk, publicly posted); (5) **IB** — buy from the Follett store for authoring reference only, write original items; (6) a **ZNotes** agreement — the CC BY-NC-SA belief is unverified and the PDF notice reads all-rights-reserved, so founder-supplied ZNotes text is treated as user-uploaded material for that user until agreed [GL still-unverified][CS §3]. Operational: provenance ledger, per-document kill switch, 24-hour takedown SLA, trademark hygiene (never "Cambridge" in a name; a non-affiliation notice) [GL §A5].

### 7.5 Data, privacy and compliance architecture

A per-tenant **policy object** `{residency, allowed_providers, retention_days, age_band, consent_mode, training_opt_in=false}` is consulted by the router and the storage layer on every request [A50]. EU/UK school tenants route Claude and Gemini through Vertex regional endpoints (+10%), never DeepSeek, with data at rest in region; Fable 5.1 (30-day retention) is excluded for ZDR tenants [A50][A58]. Age: 13+ with age assurance and parental consent, under-13 deferred [C12]; GDPR consent ages 13–16 by member state; COPPA school-consent exception and FERPA school-official role for US schools; UK AADC defaults (high privacy, no nudges, DPIA) [AR §4.7]. Provider terms: no-training API tiers; images and uploads not used for training; no training on pupil data without explicit consent [A58]. EU AI Act: Article 50 disclosure persistent since 2 Aug 2026; Annex III (evaluating learning outcomes) deferred to 2 Dec 2027 — we build the technical file now (risk management, data governance, logging, human oversight, accuracy metrics) and use it in procurement [A58][C9]. Human oversight is a product mode, not a document: consumer practice marks carry confidence bands; anything feeding a school-recorded grade requires teacher confirmation [C11]. Security: chunks and uploads are untrusted data; per-route tool allow-lists; no-network sandboxes; per-tenant vector partitions [A49][AR §4.6]. Learning receipts are exportable as evidence of authorship [A59].

### 7.6 Multi-tenant model

`orgs(type ∈ {individual, family, school, tutor_centre}, region, residency, plan)`; `users(role ∈ {student, parent, teacher, admin, tutor})`; `guardian_links` with consent evidence; `classes` bound to a Course; RLS by `org_id` [AR §4.3]. The teacher console shares the retrieval and marking services — one stack, role-gated [AR impl. 23].

### 7.7 Growth engine, pricing and unit economics

Channels in order of CAC: (1) **teacher mode free** → class codes → pilots → paid seats; measure activation per licensed school >60%, not contracts [A55][BM impl. 25]; (2) **creators** for 9609/9708/9700 with "grade my essay live" shareables, paid on 30-day-retained subscribers [A56]; (3) **communities** (r/alevel, TSR, WhatsApp/Telegram) with a free-forever past-paper finder and mark-scheme explainer (a link index, never a host) [A56]; (4) **seasonality**: spend Jan–Apr and Sep–Oct, win-back for the next series [BM §2.1]; (5) education-native referral: study-group invites, "send your teacher a report", parent digests that make the parent the payer. ### 7.8 Moats, in order of durability

Calibration data (paired scripts and examiner marks per paper, refreshed each series) > tagged item banks with live statistics > the learner graph with outcome labels (actual grades) > school trust and compliance artefacts > community/UGC > brand as the honest tutor [BM §3.1]. Not moats: the UI, the prompts, access to the same four APIs [BM §3.1]. Each moat has a flywheel inside the product: teacher overrides → calibration; every generated item → statistics; every consented result → outcome labels.

### 7.9 Three-year strategy and the org / AI-agent build plan

**Y1 — win one exam, prove the marker.** Packs for 9609/0450/9708/9709/0580 (+ 9701 mechanisms), published calibration per paper, 20 pilot schools (UK, Gulf, Kazakhstan), 30–50 creators; targets 100k MAU, 10k paying, QWK ≥0.75 pilot → 0.80 gate; pre-registered efficacy study within two exam sessions [BM §6.7][A61]. **Y2 — many curricula, schools, community.** Compiler at <4 weeks per pack; UK boards, IB, AP, one of ЕГЭ/UNT [E3]; school product ≤$12/seat; community loop; Annex III conformity ready before 2 Dec 2027; targets 1M MAU, 100k paying, 300 schools. **Y3 — category definition.** Outcome guarantees on 2–3 subjects, marker API for publishers and tutoring chains, board/ministry partnerships, LMS integrations; 3–5M MAU, 500k–1M paying, >40% revenue outside the UK [BM §6.7].

## 8. What we deliberately do NOT do (and why), and where you disagree with INDEX decisions (say so explicitly with reasons)

**We do not:** sell answers or let "the answer" be the value [A4]; host Cambridge PDFs or build a mark-scheme viewer [GL impl. 2]; say "replaces teachers" [A5]; ship daily streaks, confetti, shame notifications or ads [A40][A58]; mark anything without a scheme in context or below gate [A18][A22]; produce submittable coursework [A59]; build image/video generation, agents, deep research or a bot directory at launch [FB §6]; run DeepSeek on identifiable student data [A46]; ship voice in v1 [C13]; claim any efficacy multiplier that is not pre-registered [A7]; depend on SEO [A56]; build a separate teacher stack [AR impl. 23]; or adopt a dedicated vector DB before pgvector p95 >100 ms [A49].

**Where I disagree with INDEX, explicitly:**

1. **Second market ([A6], [E3]).** INDEX sequences CAIE → UK boards → one national exam. I would sequence by pack reuse, not geography: after CAIE consumers, go to CAIE/Edexcel-IAL international schools in the Gulf and Kazakhstan (same packs, high WTP, school distribution, the founder's network) before spending a UK-board pack build to fight SME/Medly/Seneca head-on. UK boards come once the compiler is <4 weeks per pack.
2. **Calibration second-marking on a 20% sample ([A44], [A23]).** For the first two exam series, run the cross-family second mark on 100% of levels items ≥12 marks (≈+$0.03–0.06 per essay). Calibration data is the strongest moat [BM §3.1]; sampling it to save cents in Year 1 is false economy. Drop to sampling per paper once that paper is gated.
3. **Age floor at MVP ([C12], [E4]).** INDEX resolves to 13+ consumer with parental consent from day one. In a 6-month build I would open consumer sign-up at 16+ (the AS/A Level cohort) and serve 13–15 (IGCSE) only through school tenants under the school-consent path until the consent stack passes an external audit (target month 9). Same packs, smaller compliance surface, no under-16 consumer data before the DPIA is real.
4. **Free-tier marking cap ([A54], [C7]).** Cap levels marks (Opus-class) per day, but leave points marking (Sonnet draft, ~$0.05) effectively uncapped with soft slow-down; it is the cheapest habit-former we have and the cap should be about cost, not about withholding the core [A54].
5. **Curriculum compiler timing ([BM §6.7] puts it in Y2).** The compiler is the MVP's internal tool from month 1; 0450 and 9708 are its first outputs. Building the packs by hand and the compiler later would double the content cost.

## 10. Metrics of success (north-star, guard-rails, per-feature) and the biggest risks with mitigations
