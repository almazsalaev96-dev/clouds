## 0. Executive summary

1. **LLM marking of real exam scripts is now at or above second-examiner consistency for maths and science, and close to it for essays.** The strongest 2026 evidence is a benchmark of **32,534 double-marked real GCSE mock responses across 328 questions in five subjects, including handwritten work**, where "top performing models agree more closely with examiner consensus than examiners agree with each other"; in maths the examiner–examiner QWK baseline is 0.84 and top science models beat examiners by up to ~+0.06 QWK ([arXiv 2606.24973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24973)). Agreement was "not massively discriminated by model size", i.e. mid-size models suffice for much of the workload.
2. **The human baseline is much lower than students think.** Ofqual's marking-consistency research puts the median probability of receiving the *definitive* grade at **0.52 for English language/literature up to 0.96 for Mathematics**; economics ≈74%, history ≈56% ([Ofqual 2016](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a85437de5274a2e87dc3e1c/Marking_consistency_metrics_-_November_2016.pdf), [2018 update](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5bfbfd70e5274a0fb775cca3/Marking_consistency_metrics_-_an_update_-_FINAL64492.pdf), [Study International summary](https://studyinternational.com/news/a-level-gcse-grade-right-wrong/)). Carousel Learning found teachers agree with each other only **87%** of the time on short answers ([EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/ai-carousel-research-and-findings)). Our target is therefore "as consistent as a second examiner", which is a measurable, achievable bar — not "perfect".
3. **Regulators have drawn a bright line: AI may not be the sole marker of regulated qualifications** (Ofqual, restated Jan 2026: "promising for quality assurance and marker training, but ... nowhere near ready to take over high-stakes marking") ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ofquals-approach-to-regulating-the-use-of-artificial-intelligence-in-the-qualifications-sector/ofquals-approach-to-regulating-the-use-of-artificial-intelligence-in-the-qualifications-sector--2), [Ofqual blog 14 Jan 2026](https://ofqual.blog.gov.uk/2026/01/14/using-ai-in-marking-why-technical-capability-fairness-and-transparency-all-matter/)). The IB says it "will continue to rely on human examiners" and is exploring AI as a QC layer ([IBO](https://ibo.org/news/news-about-the-ib/statement-from-the-ib-about-chatgpt-and-artificial-intelligence-in-assessment-and-education/)). AQA ran a 2024 trial comparing AI marks with senior markers but said it was "not proposing that we replace human marking" ([TES](https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/general/gcses-2024-exam-board-trial-ai-summer-exams)). This is *good* for us: boards are not going to ship a student-facing AI examiner; the formative/practice niche is open, and our product is not a regulated marker.
4. **Techniques that measurably move accuracy:** (i) putting the *real* rubric/level descriptors in context with **all levels explicitly defined** (accuracy fell progressively as 6→3→1→0 levels were defined, [medRxiv 2026](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.20.26348918v1.full)); (ii) **scored exemplar answers ("anchors")** — conditions with exemplars were more accurate than without (same study; [Anchor is the key](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1075293526000413); [example-selection study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.18924)); (iii) **data-driven rubric refinement** against ~200 human-scored samples raised QWK by up to **+0.19 (TOEFL11) and +0.47 (ASAP)** for GPT-4.1 / Gemini-2.5-Pro / Qwen3 ([arXiv 2510.09030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09030)); (iv) **pairwise comparison → scale** methods (LCES, [EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1523.pdf)) for holistic essays; (v) multi-perspective/ensemble judging ([arXiv 2509.14834](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.14834), [2601.22386](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22386)). Known failure modes: length effects (LLMs over-score short/underdeveloped essays and under-score long ones relative to humans — [arXiv 2603.23714](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23714)), leniency in some small models, position bias in pairwise judging ([IJCNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.18.pdf)), and run-to-run variance (Pack et al. 2024, cited in [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666920X26000275)).
5. **Handwriting/maths OCR is solved well enough to ship for photos of scripts, with a fallback.** A large study of real handwritten college-maths submissions found ~**88% of transcriptions acceptable** for rubric-based grading, with **GPT-4.1-mini (84%) beating Mathpix (55%)** on a hard 171-solution subset after April 2025 ([arXiv 2603.00895](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00895)). Frontier VLM character error rates on IAM handwriting are ~1.2–1.4% (GPT-5 ≈1.22%, Claude Opus 4.7 ≈1.31%, Gemini 3 ≈1.44%) ([CodeSOTA](https://www.codesota.com/ocr/best-for-handwriting)); Gemini 3 Flash is quoted at $0.004/page vs Mathpix $0.025/page ([Rivin benchmark](https://igorrivin.github.io/blog/ocr-benchmark/)).
6. **EU AI Act:** AI that *evaluates learning outcomes* is a listed high-risk use (Annex III 3(b)), with obligations applying from **2 August 2026** ("originally due" — some sources note a possible postponement; treat as live) ([artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/), [EU AI Compass](https://euaicompass.com/eu-ai-act-for-education.html)). A practice-marking tool that never feeds a certificated grade is arguably lower risk, but we should build to the high-risk transparency/human-oversight standard anyway.
7. **Cambridge 9609 specifics (cross-checked):** paper structure and AO weights in the local plan match the syllabus; the levels grid vocabulary is confirmed ("limited analysis = one link in the chain, max 3 marks; developed analysis = two or more links"; "effective evaluation 6–7 marks = developed judgement/conclusion in the business context drawing together developed evaluative comments"; explicit **best-fit** instruction) ([9609 specimen P2 MS](https://www.studocu.com/in/document/dhirubhai-ambani-institute-of-information-communication-technology/discrete-mathematics/596168-2023-specimen-paper-2-mark-scheme/37593496)), and the generic marking principles are verbatim-recoverable. Examiner reports repeat two criticisms: evaluation sections are "very limited" and "simply repeating the business name will not count as application" ([0450 m25 ER](https://www.scribd.com/document/906065620/0450-m25-er), [9609 m25 ER](https://pastpapers.papacambridge.com/directories/CAIE/CAIE-pastpapers/upload/9609_m25_er.pdf)).
8. **Public vs locked:** specimen papers/mark schemes, syllabuses, grade-threshold tables and the most recent series of past papers are on cambridgeinternational.org; Example Candidate Responses, older series, schemes of work and teacher guides sit behind the password-protected School Support Hub ([Cambridge help centre](https://help.cambridgeinternational.org/hc/en-gb/articles/19634507718034-What-support-materials-are-available)). Product should deep-link only to public URLs and ingest SSH material via teacher-uploaded files.

### A1.2 Academic AES benchmarks (ASAP, TOEFL11, PERSUADE)

- **Rater-effects study, EMIP 2026 (Jiao et al.)**: 10 LLMs (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, GPT-4o, o1, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 1.5/1.5 Pro/2.0, DeepSeek V3/R1) vs human experts; "ChatGPT 4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro and Claude 3.5 Sonnet showing high scoring accuracy and better intra-rater consistency" ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/emip.70018)). Practical takeaway: pick the frontier model per provider, and measure *intra*-rater consistency (re-mark the same script) as a first-class metric.
- **Reflect-and-Revise rubric refinement (2025)**: using ~200 human-scored essays, the model iteratively rewrites the rubric by reflecting on its own rationales vs. human scores; QWK gains up to +0.19 (TOEFL11) and +0.47 (ASAP) with GPT-4.1, Gemini-2.5-Pro and Qwen-3-Next-80B ([arXiv 2510.09030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09030)). This is the single most transferable technique for us: we have real mark schemes *and* (via Example Candidate Responses / teacher uploads) real marked scripts, so we can run exactly this loop per question type.
- **Anchors / few-shot**: "Anchor is the key" (2026) shows pre-scored exemplar essays ("anchors") in the prompt are what make prompting-only AES accessible ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1075293526000413)); example *selection* matters (GPT models, [arXiv 2411.18924](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.18924)); a bandit-driven prompt-selection framework reduces cost while keeping QWK ([arXiv 2608.23814](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23814)).
- **Level descriptors**: a March 2026 medRxiv study of reflective-essay scoring found accuracy "progressively decreased as 6, 3, 1, and 0 rubric levels were explicitly defined", and exemplar conditions beat no-exemplar conditions; the best prompt carried (1) the task, (2) the analytic rubric used by humans, (3) five exemplars with expert rationales ([medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.20.26348918v1.full)). Directly supports "put the full Cambridge levels grid plus ECR exemplars with commentary in context".
- **Pairwise comparison → scores (LCES, EMNLP 2025)**: zero-shot pairwise comparisons converted to a continuous scale; reported as competitive with direct scoring on ASAP/TOEFL11 and more robust than direct prompting ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1523.pdf), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.08498)). "Is GPT-4 alone sufficient?" (L@S 2024) also framed comparative judgement around rater cognition ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3657604.3664703)). No More Marking's own AI-comparative-judgement write-ups did not surface in search this session (prior knowledge: NMM published blog posts in 2024–25 reporting GPT-4-class models doing comparative judgement of primary/secondary writing at reliability close to human judges — unverified).
- **Multi-agent / dialectical**: "LLM agents at the roundtable" (multi-perspective, dialectical reasoning) and "Specialists or generalists?" (multi-agent vs single-agent essay grading) report gains from role-split judging ([arXiv 2509.14834](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.14834), [arXiv 2601.22386](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22386)). Multimodal essay benchmark EssayJudge exists for image-grounded prompts ([arXiv 2502.11916](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11916)). Adding linguistic features to LLM AES helps on ASAP ([arXiv 2502.09497](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09497)).

### A1.3 Failure modes (with sources)

| Failure mode | Evidence | Mitigation for us |
|---|---|---|
| **Length effects** (direction differs from humans) | "compared to human raters, LLMs tend to assign higher scores to short or underdeveloped essays, while assigning lower scores to longer essays" ([arXiv 2603.23714](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.23714)); feature-weighting analysis of LLM AES ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666920X26000305)) | Mark *per level criterion* not holistically; require quoted evidence for every credited point; calibrate against length-stratified gold set |
| **Leniency** | Small models "score leniently and uniformly high regardless of cell" (GPT-5.4 Mini example) ([Adaline](https://www.adaline.ai/blog/llm-as-a-judge-reliability-bias)) | Use frontier tier for levels-based AO3/AO4; monitor mean-mark drift vs gold |
| **Position bias** (pairwise) | 15 judges, ~150k instances, bias "varies significantly across judges and tasks and is not due to random chance" ([IJCNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.18.pdf)) | Swap order and average; only use pairwise as a *secondary* check |
| **Run-to-run inconsistency** | Pack et al. 2024 "notable fluctuations" across runs; stochastic decoding ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666920X26000275)) | temperature 0, structured output, self-consistency (n=3, median) for high-mark items |
| **Keyword over-crediting / surface features** | AI "flags spelling but misses argument nuance and subject reasoning" ([Structural Learning](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/ai-marking-and-feedback)); LLMs weight features unlike humans ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666920X26000305)) | Chain-of-analysis detection: credit AO3 only when a *link* (cause→effect) is quoted; apply the "cover the business name" test for AO2 |
| **Homogenisation / AI-text bias** | LLM judges bias when evaluating AI-generated text ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666920X26000275)); structural convergence in AI-augmented essays ([arXiv 2603.21228](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.21228)) | Do not reward template phrasing; gold set must include student-written, not model-written, answers |
| **First-language bias** | Cross-prompt TOEFL study of an open-weight model ([arXiv 2607.14605](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14605)) | Stratify gold set by L1/region (Cambridge cohorts are global) |

## A3. Handwriting and maths OCR for student scripts

- **Best current evidence on real messy scripts**: college handwritten mathematics, thousands of submissions: Mathpix was the most reliable choice through winter 2025; **GPT-4.1-mini with OCR-specific prompting** produced better transcriptions after April 2025; overall ~**88% acceptable** transcriptions (correct or sufficient for rubric-based grading); on a hard 171-solution subset **84% (GPT-4.1-mini) vs 55% (Mathpix)** ([arXiv 2603.00895](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00895)). Earlier: GPT-4V ">85% consistency on direct-answer questions" for university handwritten maths exams ([aimultiple](https://aimultiple.com/handwriting-recognition)).
- **General handwriting**: IAM CER — GPT-5 ≈1.22%, Claude Opus 4.7 ≈1.31%, Gemini 3 ≈1.44% (vs GPT-4o 1.69% in Mar 2025) ([CodeSOTA](https://www.codesota.com/ocr/best-for-handwriting)); on 100 cursive samples by 10 writers Gemini 3 Pro Preview scored 100% vs DeepSeek-OCR 79% ([aimultiple](https://aimultiple.com/handwriting-recognition)); tool round-ups ([Suparse](https://www.suparse.com/blog/best-handwriting-ocr-tools-2026), [aimultiple OCR](https://aimultiple.com/ocr-accuracy)).
- **Maths-specific cost/quality**: Gemini 3 Flash $0.004/page vs Mathpix $0.025/page and "more accurate output for mathematical content" per one independent benchmark ([Rivin](https://igorrivin.github.io/blog/ocr-benchmark/)). Answer-sheet OCR survey ([Preprints.org](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202510.1533)); handwritten-code recognition for CS education ([arXiv 2408.07220](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.07220v1)).
- **Diagrams** (Ansoff/BCG grids, break-even charts, network diagrams, curly-arrow mechanisms): no benchmark found; the GCSE benchmark included handwritten maths scripts and reported good agreement, but mechanism-diagram marking (M1 "lone pair AND curly arrow from lone pair to carbonyl C") is conjunctive and geometric — treat as **unsolved, human-confirm** until we build a gold set. (Prior knowledge, unverified: Azure Document Intelligence / Textract are strong on printed forms and weaker on maths; Pen-to-Print targets cursive; none is designed for chemistry mechanisms.)

**Design consequence**: two-pass ingestion — (1) VLM transcription with an OCR-specific prompt that preserves layout, crossings-out and working; (2) the transcript *and* the image go to the marker, which must quote the transcript when crediting and must flag "illegible/uncertain" tokens rather than guessing. Student confirms the transcript before marking (removes ~10% of avoidable errors for free).

## A4. Evaluation methodology and target numbers for our AI examiner

### A4.1 Gold sets (what "truth" is)
1. **Tier 0 — official**: Example Candidate Responses (ECRs: real scripts + examiner marks + commentary, per paper) and Specimen Paper Answers (Cambridge publishes these for 9609 P1–P4, e.g. the [P4 specimen answers PDF](https://www.learnedguys.com/uploads/files/2097/9609_Specimen_Paper_Answers_Paper_4_(for_examination_from_2023).pdf) mirrored publicly; ECR booklets "contain examples of marked student answers ... with commentary from senior examiners" ([Cambridge help](https://help.cambridgeinternational.org/hc/en-gb/articles/19634507718034-What-support-materials-are-available))). Typically 3 scripts per question (high/middle/low). Small but authoritative — use as *seeds*, never for training only.
2. **Tier 1 — teacher double-marked**: partner-school scripts marked by two teachers with the official scheme; keep both marks (gives a human–human agreement line per question, exactly as the Medly benchmark did).
3. **Tier 2 — synthetic-but-human-marked**: model-written answers at targeted levels, *human-marked*; use only for robustness (length, template phrasing), never for headline metrics (AI-text bias).
4. Stratify by: question type (points vs levels), marks (2/3/5/8/12/20), command word, paper, series, region/L1, handwritten vs typed, answer length.

### A4.2 Metrics (per question type, not one number)
- **Points-based items (Define/State/Calculate/M-A-B marks)**: exact agreement, MAE, and *per-marking-point* precision/recall (did it credit M1 but not M2?). Target: exact agreement ≥ human–human exact agreement on the same items; per-point F1 ≥ 0.9.
- **Levels-based items (8/12/20 marks)**: QWK vs definitive mark; % within tolerance (±1 on 8-mark, ±2 on 12–20-mark — mirroring board tolerances, *prior knowledge, unverified*); **level agreement** per AO (did it pick the same AO3 level and AO4 level?); *rationale validity* (human audit: does every credited point quote a real, correctly interpreted span?).
- **Consistency**: intra-rater (re-mark same script ×3, temp 0 and temp default) — target ≥ 95% identical total, 100% identical level; **order/position invariance** for any pairwise step.
- **Drift/leniency**: mean signed error vs gold per week per model version; alert at |bias| > 0.5 mark on 20-mark items; re-run gold set on every model/prompt change (CI gate).
- **Fairness**: agreement by region/L1/handwriting/length bucket; no bucket may lag the overall QWK by > 0.05.

### A4.4 Process rules (board-style QA, translated)
- **Standardisation**: before a prompt/model version marks anything, it must mark the seed set (ECRs + 20 teacher-marked scripts per question family) within tolerance.
- **Seeding in production**: 2–5% of live markings are gold items re-injected; out-of-tolerance → auto-pause that question family for that model, fall back to a second model + "provisional" badge.
- **Two-marker mode for high stakes** (mock exams, predicted grades): two different providers mark independently; disagreements > tolerance escalate to a third model, then to a teacher queue.
- **Transparency (EU AI Act Annex III 3(b), Ofqual "explainable and challengeable")**: every mark carries the rubric level chosen, quoted evidence, and a one-click "challenge this mark" that re-marks with a different model and logs the case; students told plainly that marks are *practice estimates, not board marks*.
- **Human-in-the-loop**: teachers can override; overrides feed the refinement loop; never present an AI mark as a predicted grade without teacher sign-off.

### B1.2 How levels grids split marks (confirmed vocabulary)
From the published 9609 specimen/2023 mark schemes ([specimen P2 MS](https://www.studocu.com/in/document/dhirubhai-ambani-institute-of-information-communication-technology/discrete-mathematics/596168-2023-specimen-paper-2-mark-scheme/37593496); [P4 MS June 2023](https://www.studocu.com/row/document/machakos-university/business-statistics-ii/9609-s23-ms-41-9609-s23-ms-41/82278597)):
- **AO3 Analysis** — "Limited analysis shows one link in the chain of analysis (max 3 marks)"; "Developed analysis shows two or more links in the chain of analysis".
- **AO4 Evaluation** — "Limited evaluation includes unsupported judgement and/or a weak attempt at evaluative comment"; "Effective evaluation (6–7 marks): a developed judgement/conclusion made in the business context that draws together developed evaluative comments which balance some key arguments in the business context".
- **Best-fit rule (verbatim)**: "The examiner should look at the work and make a judgement about which level statement is the best fit. In practice, work does not always match one level statement precisely so a judgement may need to be made between two or more level statements."

(Prior knowledge, unverified — consistent with the confirmed "max 3" and "6–7" anchors and with the local plan's derived 3/2/8/7 split) **20-mark P4 essay grid**: AO1 Knowledge 3 (L2 2–3 developed, L1 1 limited) · AO2 Application 2 (L2 2 developed, L1 1 limited) · AO3 Analysis 8 (L2 developed 4–8, L1 limited 1–3) · AO4 Evaluation 7 (L3 effective 6–7, L2 developed 3–5, L1 limited 1–2). **12-mark grid**: AO1 2 · AO2 2 · AO3 2 · AO4 6 (the examiner-report snippet "knowledge, application, and analysis carrying just 2 marks each, while evaluative comments, conclusions and judgements can be awarded up to 6 marks" describes exactly this 2/2/2/6 shape, [0450 m25 ER](https://www.scribd.com/document/906065620/0450-m25-er)). **8-mark "Analyse" grid**: AO1 2 · AO2 2 · AO3 4, no AO4 (writing evaluation earns nothing — the local plan's "highest-frequency avoidable error").

Marking mechanics that follow: (1) each AO is levelled *independently* and marks are summed; (2) "developed" is defined by *links in a chain*, not by word count — this is the anti-length-bias rule we must encode; (3) application is credited only when the point is tied to something true of *this* business.

### (b) Worked examples of the AI examiner's output

**Example 1 — 9609 Paper 4, 20 marks, "Evaluate whether [Firm] should pursue market development rather than diversification."** Student answer (summarised): defines Ansoff, gives two paragraphs of analysis, one with a single link ("market development is less risky so profits will rise"), one with a full chain tied to the case ("the $40 000 retained profit constraint means diversification's set-up costs would need debt, and with gearing already at 68% lenders may refuse or charge more, which would cut the thin 4% margin further"), then a conclusion "both options have merits; it depends".

```
MARK: 11/20   (AO1 3/3 · AO2 2/2 · AO3 5/8 · AO4 1/7)   confidence: high on AO1–AO3, medium on AO4

AO1 Knowledge — Level 2 (3/3). Evidence: "Ansoff's matrix classifies growth by product/market newness…" [quoted lines 2–4] — accurate, developed (defines both strategies and the risk gradient).
AO2 Application — Level 2 (2/2). Evidence: "$40 000 retained profit", "gearing already at 68%", "4% margin" [lines 11–13] — facts only true of this business, used to drive the argument. (Line 7 "this would help [Firm] grow" is NOT application — would fail the cover-the-name test.)
AO3 Analysis — Level 2, low in band (5/8). Chain 1 [lines 11–14]: constraint → debt → gearing → cost of borrowing → margin = 4 links: developed. Chain 2 [line 7]: "less risky so profits rise" = 1 link: limited. Best fit: developed analysis present but only one developed chain, and no analysis of market development itself → 5, not 7–8.
AO4 Evaluation — Level 1 (1/7). "Both options have merits; it depends" [line 18] is an unsupported judgement (mark scheme: "unsupported judgement and/or weak attempt at evaluative comment"). No factor is weighed, no condition from the case, no committed recommendation.

To reach Level 3 AO4 (6–7): commit ("On balance [Firm] should pursue market development because…"), state why the decisive factor (gearing/finance constraint) outweighs the strongest counter (dependence on one product line), and attach a case condition ("provided the export market's tariff regime is stable; if not, licensing becomes preferable"). Two further AO3 marks available by building a second developed chain on the market-development side (e.g. existing brand → lower promotion cost → faster payback within the owner's 3-year sale horizon).
Sources: mark scheme levels 9609/41 (levels grid); examiner report: "very limited evaluative sections"; "repeating the business name will not count as application".
```

**Example 2 — 9609 Paper 1 Section A, 5 marks total: 1(a) "Define the term 'opportunity cost'." [2]; 1(b) "Explain one benefit to [Firm] of being a social enterprise." [3]**

## Implications for our product (concrete rules/features)

1. **Promise "as consistent as a second examiner", never "the examiner".** Display: "AI mark 11/20 (range 10–12). Real examiners disagree by ±2 on essays like this." Cite Ofqual reliability figures in a help page.
2. **Mark by AO and by level, never holistically.** Grid engine iterates AO blocks independently; each block must quote evidence spans; "developed" = counted causal links (≥2) not words; apply best-fit only within an AO.
3. **Rubric-in-context is mandatory**: full official levels grid + generic principles + command-word definition + indicative content + 3–5 ECR exemplars with commentary for that question family. Never mark from the model's memory of "what Cambridge wants".
4. **Reflect-and-Revise calibration loop per question family**: hold out 200 human-marked scripts; iteratively refine the *marker instructions* (not the official grid) until QWK plateaus; version and gate.
5. **Determinism controls**: temperature 0, structured JSON output, n=3 self-consistency for ≥12-mark items (median AO marks), model/provider ensemble for disputed items; log per-run variance.
6. **Anti-bias checks baked in**: (a) cover-the-business-name test for AO2; (b) evaluation-in-Analyse detection → "0 marks available, here's why"; (c) length-stratified regression tests; (d) order swap for any pairwise judgement.
7. **Points engine speaks Cambridge**: M/A/B/DM/DB/FT/CAO/OE/ISW/SOI/CWO/AG are first-class fields; ECF computed from the candidate's own prior values; sig-fig penalty once per paper.
8. **Handwriting flow**: VLM transcription (frontier model, OCR prompt) → student confirms/corrects transcript → mark from transcript + image; illegible tokens flagged, never guessed; mechanism/diagram items default to "provisional — teacher confirm" until the diagram gold set exists.
9. **QA like a board**: standardisation on seed set before any version goes live; 2–5% live seeding; tolerance breaches auto-pause the question family; dashboards for bias/drift per model version.
10. **Transparency & oversight (Ofqual principles / EU AI Act Annex III 3(b))**: rationale always visible; "challenge this mark" button (re-mark with a different provider, human queue if still disputed); teacher override feeds calibration; no AI mark ever flows into a predicted grade without teacher sign-off; data-governance and logging documented as if high-risk.
11. **Source discipline**: deep-link only to cambridgeinternational.org public PDFs (syllabus, specimen, thresholds, latest series); SSH-only material (ECRs, older series, confidential instructions) enters via teacher upload and is processed per-user; third-party notes ranked below scheme/ER/syllabus.
12. **Feedback = examiner-report language**: every "next mark" tip is phrased as Cambridge ERs phrase it ("candidates who scored well…"), with the ER cited, so students learn the marker's model of a good answer — the local plan's core insight.
13. **Grade-threshold context**: after a full paper, show component mark vs the last three series' thresholds (public PDFs), labelled historical.
