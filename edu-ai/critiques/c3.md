# Critique c3

## Verdict

Not signable as an executable build spec, though the *policy* thinking in §06, §12 and §08.7–08.9 is genuinely strong and mostly ready. Three classes of defect stop an AI coding agent dead. First, the document is not self-consistent at the level of **types**: the same four fields (`policy_version`, `pack_version`, item and mark ids, `gate_state`/`intent`/`mastery_state` enums) appear in four or five mutually incompatible formats across §05.2, §08, §11.2, §12.2, §12.9 and §15.C, and §15.C.5/§15.C.7 declare `additionalProperties: false` over shapes that the document's own worked examples violate — so a builder who validates as instructed cannot ship any of the examples. Second, §14.5's north-star computation — the one metric the whole KPI tree hangs on — is an **empty code block**; the stitch dropped it, along with six `<!-- END gN -->` editor markers left in the shipped text. Third, the money does not reconcile: §08.10 and §14.6 model the same personas with different volumes and reach different answers (school seat $6.58/yr vs $2.90/yr against a $10 price and a ≥70% margin target), the levels-Mark unit cost assumes 20% second-marking in a year when §08.6 mandates 100%, and the default tenant budget ($1.20/seat/month) exceeds the seat price.

Fix first, in this order: (1) restore §14.5 and strip the stitch markers; (2) make §15.C the single normative type source and regenerate every inline example from it, with one ID/version grammar table in §00; (3) reconcile the quota table across §03.3 / §08.8 / §11-COST-001 into one `limits` file and regenerate; (4) resolve the DeepSeek policy contradiction (§06.2 forbids LICENSED text, §08-RT-019 permits it) and define the `pii`/`pii_class`/`job_class` fields the enforcement depends on but that exist nowhere; (5) rebuild §14.6 from §08.10's unit costs at MVP-year second-marking rates. The licensing posture is the best part of the document and needs only the extract/quotation `licence_basis` gap closed; the privacy posture is strong on GDPR/AADC and thin on the AI Act obligations that fall on Margin as *provider* rather than deployer.
