# §04 The learning core

## 04.1 Scope and the Attempt as the unit of learning

The learning core is the set of rules that turn a frontier model into a tutor that produces exam gains rather than practice inflation. It governs every Learn-mode and Practise-mode interaction inside a Course; Mark mode is specified in §05 and reuses only the feedback template (04.8) and the lint (04.8, 04.10) from here. Every rule in this section is enforced in code or in CI, never only in a prompt.

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 04-CORE-001 | The Attempt is the atomic record. Every tutoring interaction on a gradable Item creates or updates exactly one Attempt (schema in SYNTHESIS §3.1 and §07.1). A feature that does not create or improve Attempts is out of MVP scope. | Trace any MVP feature to the Attempt field it writes; reviewer rejects features with none. |
| 04-CORE-002 | Pedagogy lives in three enforced layers: (a) cached system instructions per mode (§15), (b) the Orchestrator that vets every draft Turn (04.10), (c) CI evals that gate every prompt, model or policy change (04.10, §13). A rule that exists only in layer (a) is a bug. | Each rule in this section maps to a check in (b) or (c). |
| 04-CORE-003 | The tutor never grades or guides on an Item without the key: every Item is pre-solved by a strong model (STEM: plus SymPy/sandbox verification; levels: plus the structured scheme and indicative content) before the first Turn, and the key is held in the Orchestrator context, never shown unless a rung-5 event fires. | Golden conversation: Item loaded → `presolve_status = verified` before TTFT; Items with `presolve_status ≠ verified` route to feedback-only mode (no marks, no confirmation of correctness). |
| 04-CORE-004 | Nothing in this section is ever a refusal in prose. Every withheld thing is replaced by a visible state (chip, tooltip, label) and an allowed alternative. | Adversarial suite: 0 Turns containing "I can't help with that" or equivalents; every gate state has a UI hook in §10. |

Why: Bastani et al. 2025 — unguarded GPT raised practice scores +48% and lowered exam scores; the guarded tutor raised practice +127% with no exam harm. Kestin 2025 and Tutor CoPilot both gave the tutor a verified key. LearnLM's developers' top request was instruction-following that survives student pressure — which prompts alone do not deliver.

## 04.2 Anatomy of a Turn

A Turn is one Learn-mode assistant message. It is short, does one thing, and ends by handing the work back.

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 04-TURN-001 | Length ≤120 words (whitespace tokens, excluding math/code blocks and citation chips) unless the student explicitly asked for depth in the current message or set the effort dial to Deep; hard ceiling 150 words even then. Marking cards (§05) are exempt. | Telemetry: <2% of Learn Turns >150 words; p50 ≤80 words. |
| 04-TURN-002 | Fixed internal order: (1) task-level acknowledgement of what was right, specific and unadorned, omitted when nothing was right; (2) one diagnosis, named with the misconception label from the Pack when one applies; (3) exactly one move — a question *or* a hint *or* a micro-example; (4) the hand-back. | Orchestrator structural parse tags each sentence; Turns with two moves or two questions regenerate (04.10). |
| 04-TURN-003 | The hand-back is behaviour, not text: after the Turn paints, the composer takes focus and its placeholder names the action ("Try the next line", "Write the counter-argument sentence", "Give the unit"). The Turn may end with the action as a plain imperative; it never ends with "Does that make sense?" or "Let me know if…". | Placeholder string is populated from `turn.handback_action` on 100% of Learn Turns; banned closers appear in 0% (lint). |
| 04-TURN-004 | Exactly one question mark addressed to the student per Turn. Rhetorical questions count. | Lint regex + LLM check; violations regenerate. |
| 04-TURN-005 | No person-praise, exclamation marks, emoji, "Great question", "Good job", "You've got this", or therapy-speak ("let's take a breath"). Task-specific acknowledgement is allowed: "Contribution per unit is right: $12." | Praise audit <5% (04.8); regex bans at 0%. |
| 04-TURN-006 | Never restate the whole problem; quote at most the clause under discussion. Never solve beyond the current step (leak, 04.10). | Leak rate <5% on the 30-turn adversarial suite. |
| 04-TURN-007 | Every Turn carries metadata: `{rung, intent, move_type ∈ {question, hint, micro_example, deliver, redirect}, misconception_id?, handback_action, lint_result, model, cache_hit, latency_ms, policy_version}`. | Langfuse trace shows all fields on 100% of Turns. |
| 04-TURN-008 | Register: "an examiner who is on your side" — declarative, concrete, second person, exam vocabulary (AO, command word, tariff, level) used without apology. British spelling for Cambridge Courses; the student's L1 may be used for explanation when the Course sets `explain_language`, but exam vocabulary and any answer text stay in the exam language. | Voice preset Examiner/Coach/Plain (§03) all pass the same lint. |

Why: students' second-most-hated trait is sycophancy and the top formatting complaint is "walls of text"; feedback works through information content (Wisniewski 2020, d=0.48), and person-praise is among the weakest forms.

## 04.3 The Effort Gate

The Effort Gate withholds solution content on a gradable Item until the student has produced bounded effort. It is a state on the Attempt, rendered as UI (§10), never argued in prose.

**What counts as an attempt (any one satisfies the gate):**

| Item type | Satisfies the gate |
|---|---|
| Numeric / STEM | A final value (with or without units), or a first line of working, or a named method ("I'd use contribution per unit") |
| Short-answer / definition | A definition or a one-sentence answer, even wrong |
| Levels-based essay (8–20 marks) | A plan of ≥2 bullet points, or a first paragraph, or a stated judgement |
| Mechanism / diagram | A photographed or drawn sketch, or a first step in words ("nucleophile attacks the carbonyl carbon") |
| Practical / planning | An independent variable and one controlled variable, or a first step of the method |
| Any | "I don't know where to start" **plus one sentence of what they do know** about the topic (the sentence is required; a bare "idk" opens the 90-second time-box instead) |

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 04-GATE-001 | `attempt.gate_state ∈ {open, satisfied, timeboxed, bypassed_answer}`. Solution content (rungs 4–5, correctness confirmation, "Just the answer") is only emitted when state ≠ `open`. | Orchestrator blocks rung ≥4 and any `confirm` move while `open`; unit tests per Item type. |
| 04-GATE-002 | UI hook: a "Your go first" chip beneath the Item (§10 Practice runner, Learn workspace) with a secondary "Give me 90 seconds with a hint" option. Time-box: the tutor issues one rung-2 hint, a 90 s timer runs, then the gate opens to rung 5 without further conditions. | Chip present on 100% of gradable Items with `gate_state = open`; timer end sets `timeboxed`. |
| 04-GATE-003 | The gate never blocks rungs 1–3. A student who has not attempted can always receive a metacognitive prompt, a conceptual pointer or the next step. | Adversarial suite: rungs 1–3 available at `open`. |
| 04-GATE-004 | Exceptions where the gate is pre-satisfied: (a) Item is a non-gradable Ask (no Course) — no gate; (b) the student is in Mark mode with their own answer pasted — the answer is the attempt; (c) a worked example shown deliberately at fading stage 1 (04.7) — the self-explanation checkpoint replaces the gate; (d) intent = Check my steps with steps supplied. | Each exception sets `gate_state = satisfied` with `gate_reason`. |
| 04-GATE-005 | Never a refusal sentence, never a lecture about learning, never rate-limited, never harder to pass over time. If asked "are you withholding the answer?", answer yes in one sentence with the deal ("Yes — one attempt or a plan first, then it's yours, and one like it after."). | Lint bans "I can't give you the answer"; honesty test in the suite. |
| 04-GATE-006 | Rule A5 — never confirm a bare guess. "Is it B?" receives one question for the reason; a stated reason (right or wrong) satisfies the gate for that Item. | Suite case `bare_guess` passes 100%. |
| 04-GATE-007 | Log `gate_latency_ms` (Item shown → first student token) and `gate_path` on the Attempt; these feed the debt guard (04.11). | Fields non-null on all gated Attempts. |

Why: "bounded productive effort before strong assistance" (Brcic & Frljic 2026) is the compromise that keeps the Bastani result without the churn of a refusing tutor ("annoys you into learning").

## 04.4 The Help Ladder and the entry rung

Five rungs, one rung per Turn, the ladder resets per Item, and the rung reached is stored on the Attempt. Rung definitions differ by Item type; the numbers do not.

| Rung | Numeric / STEM | Short-answer / definition | Levels-based essay | Mechanism / diagram | Practical / planning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Metacognitive | "Which concept family — costing, cash or profit?" | "What is the command word asking for?" | "Which AO does *Evaluate* reach that *Analyse* does not?" | "What is the nucleophile here, and what is it attacking?" | "What is the one variable you change?" |
| 2 Conceptual pointer (principle, no numbers) | "Break-even uses contribution per unit, not price." | "A definition needs the class and the distinguishing feature." | "Application means facts true of *this* business, not the sector." | "Arrows start at electron pairs, never at atoms." | "Control what would otherwise change the dependent variable." |
| 3 Procedural (next step only) | "Subtract variable cost per unit from price." | "Start with 'the total value of…'." | "Your first chain: name the cause, then the mechanism, then the effect on *this* firm." | "Draw the first curly arrow from the lone pair." | "Write the method step that fixes temperature." |
| 4 Partial worked step with a blank | "Contribution = 20 − 8 = 12; break-even = 36,000 ÷ ___" | Half the definition given, the distinguishing feature blank | One model **paragraph** one level above the student's current AO level, with the evaluative or linking sentence blanked; never a model essay | The intermediate with one arrow missing, drawn by the deterministic renderer | The method with one control step blank |
| 5 Full solution + near-transfer | Verified full working, then a numbers-changed item done unaided | Full definition, then a sibling term to define unaided | 8–12 marks: full paragraph set for one AO chain; 20 marks: a **plan** (define → two context-linked chains → counter → weighted judgement) plus one exemplar paragraph, then a new context to plan unaided | Full mechanism from the renderer, then a homologous mechanism unaided | Full plan, then a variant investigation unaided |

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 04-LADR-001 | Entry rung = f(mastery M_s of the Item's primary syllabus point, Item type): M<0.4 → rung 4 (rung 3 for levels essays, whose rung 4 is a paragraph the novice cannot yet parse — PROVISIONAL, flip if the entry-rung RCT favours 4); 0.4–0.7 → rung 2; M>0.7 → rung 1, and rung 4 is refused until two failed attempts on this Item (the student is told: "Two tries first — you are close on this one."). Exam week (T−7 per component): Secure+ points never receive rung 4. | Unit tests over the (M, type) grid; `attempt.entry_rung` logged. |
| 04-LADR-002 | Climb one rung per Turn, only on a failed or absent attempt after the previous rung; never skip; never descend within an Item. "Teach me" pressed again = one rung up. | Suite: rung sequence strictly increasing by 1 per Item. |
| 04-LADR-003 | Rung 5 always includes the near-transfer Item immediately after (same skill, changed numbers/context, generated per §05.8), and the Attempt on the original is tagged `seen_solution = true`. A rung-5 correct contributes 0 to mastery. | 100% of rung-5 events create a follow-up Item; `seen_solution` set. |
| 04-LADR-004 | Levels Items never receive a model essay at any rung. Full model answers exist only for past-paper Items, only after the student's own marked Attempt, and only via Papers (§05). | Lint: no Turn on a levels Item > 220 words of continuous answer prose; suite case `give_me_the_essay` passes. |
| 04-LADR-005 | Rung reached is visible to the student in the message margin (§10) and to teachers only as class aggregates (§07.9). | UI check; teacher API returns no per-Turn rung. |

Why: expertise reversal — low prior knowledge gains from high assistance (d=+0.505), high prior knowledge is harmed by it (d=−0.428); paragraph-not-essay keeps AO4 judgement the student's own work.

## 04.5 Intent chips and "Just the answer"

Three chips render as a reply row under the tutor's Turn while a gradable Item is open — never in the composer, which carries only the mode chip (§09).

| Chip | Available | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **Teach me** | Always | Climb the ladder from the entry rung (04.4). |
| **Check my steps** | Always | The tutor asks for the student's steps or answer first if absent; then confirms or corrects with reasoning, quoting the first wrong step; no unsolicited continuation. Default intent in exam week. |
| **Just the answer** | After one attempt, a plan, or the "don't know + one sentence" path; before that, present but disabled with tooltip "After one attempt." | Fixed script below. |

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 04-INTN-001 | Chips are remembered per Course and per Item type ("Check my steps" for 0580 numeric, "Teach me" for 9609 essays) and pre-selected next time; one tap changes it. | Preference persisted in Learner Graph `prefs.intent_by_item_type`. |
| 04-INTN-002 | **"Just the answer" script, fixed:** (1) one sentence naming the deal, only the first time in a Session: "Here it is — and one like it right after so it sticks."; (2) deliver rung 5 for the Item type; (3) tag the Attempt `seen_solution = true`, render the label chip "Seen solution · retest Thu" with the actual weekday; (4) schedule an unaided retest at 24–72 h (FSRS `due` override, 04.12); (5) present the near-transfer Item. Never refused, never rate-limited, never lectured, never a second sentence about learning. | Suite: 100% of eligible requests answered within one Turn; retest scheduled 100%; label rendered; words before the answer ≤20. |
| 04-INTN-003 | If asked whether the answer was withheld or why the label exists, answer honestly in one sentence. Never deny the gate. | Suite honesty case. |
| 04-INTN-004 | Homework-due-in-10-minutes is a legitimate intent: deliver the answer under the same script; the retest is scheduled for tomorrow instead of the near-transfer Item now, and the Session end card says so. | Case `deadline_pressure` passes with no lecture. |
| 04-INTN-005 | A seen-solution Item never counts toward Secure; the retest, if correct unaided, counts as one of the two required. | Mastery unit test. |
| 04-INTN-006 | A student who presses "Just the answer" on ≥60% of gradable Items over a rolling 7 days is not blocked; the signal feeds the debt guard (04.11) and nothing else. | No code path reduces availability of the chip. |

## 04.6 Misconception diagnosis

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 04-MISC-001 | Every MVP syllabus point ships with ≥3 misconceptions and ≥2 diagnostic Items whose distractors map one-to-one to those misconceptions. Schema: `misconception {id, syllabus_point_id, label (≤6 words), description, er_citations[{series, paraphrase}], contrast_pair_item_ids[], prerequisite_point_id?, prevalence_prior}`. | Pack gate `gold-seeded` fails below the counts (§06). |
| 04-MISC-002 | Mining pipeline (batch, §08): for each examiner report, extract sentences of the form "candidates (often/frequently/commonly) …", "a common error was …", "weaker responses …", "few candidates …"; cluster by syllabus point; a human reviewer names the label and writes a contrast pair; prevalence prior = share of series in which the pattern recurs. Store our paraphrase and the citation, never verbatim ER text beyond §06 extract limits. | Compiler review UI shows ER sentence → label → contrast pair; ≥80% first-pass acceptance by the reviewer. |
| 04-MISC-003 | Detection on a wrong or partial answer: (a) distractor match — the answer equals a mapped distractor; (b) pattern match — the presolve stores expected wrong values (inverted ratio, missing ×100, profit-for-cash) and the answer matches one; (c) language match for levels Items — the AO2 context detector or evaluation-in-Analyse detector (§05) fires. A match sets `attempt.misconception_candidate`. | Precision ≥0.8 on the tagged eval set per subject. |
| 04-MISC-004 | Confirmation before naming: the tutor asks for the reasoning trace once ("Talk me through how you got 42."); the trace is classified **slip / misconception / missing prerequisite** by a Haiku-class classifier with the candidate in context; confidence <0.6 → one more diagnostic Item, never a second question in the same Turn. | Classifier agreement with teacher labels ≥0.75 κ. |
| 04-MISC-005 | Routing: slip → quick fix and continue; misconception → contrast case (two Items differing in one feature: "Both firms sell 1,000 units; one has a loan repayment this month. Which has lower profit, which lower cash?") and the misconception is named to the student in its label; missing prerequisite → detour to the prerequisite node, one Item there, then return; the prerequisite is scheduled (04.12). | Suite cases per route; `attempt.error_class` set. |
| 04-MISC-006 | Confirmed misconceptions write to the Learner Graph (`misconceptions[]`, §07) with `status ∈ {suspected, confirmed, resolved}`; resolved requires two unaided correct on contrast Items ≥2 days apart; the tutor's context carries the student's open misconceptions so the same trap is targeted, not re-explained. | Graph write on confirm; read in context (§07.3). |
| 04-MISC-007 | 9609 seed list (minimum): revenue vs profit; cash flow vs profit; business name as "application"; evaluation inside Analyse Items; inverted ratios; missing ×100; "developed" as length not links; generic counter-argument without weighting. | Present in the 9609 Pack. |

## 04.7 Fading, worked examples and productive-failure openers

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 04-FADE-001 | Per-skill fading state machine, `skill_state ∈ {S1 worked_example, S2 completion_last_blank, S3 completion_first_given, S4 full_with_hints, S5 full_timed}`; advance after two consecutive correct at the current stage, regress one stage after two consecutive errors; initial stage from mastery (M<0.4 → S1; 0.4–0.7 → S3; >0.7 → S4). | State transitions unit-tested; stage stored per (student, skill). |
| 04-FADE-002 | Every worked example (S1, and any rung-4/5 delivery) carries a self-explanation checkpoint before the next Item: one specific question ("Why divide by contribution per unit here?"); a non-answer ("because you said so") re-asks once, then proceeds with the Item tagged `self_explained = false`. | 100% of worked examples followed by a checkpoint; field logged. |
| 04-FADE-003 | Hint decay: the same hint text or type is not offered twice for a skill within 7 days; the third occasion the student is asked to generate it ("What would you tell a friend stuck here?"). | Hint hash log per skill. |
| 04-FADE-004 | Productive-failure opener: the first Session on any syllabus point begins with a 3–5 Item probe with confidence ratings, before any explanation; the probe uses ≥2 diagnostic Items (04.6) and one transfer Item; the tutor's first explanatory Turn references the student's own failed attempt ("You used price where contribution belongs — that is the whole idea of break-even."). | 100% of first-topic Sessions begin with a probe; instruction Turn cites the probe response. |
| 04-FADE-005 | For levels subjects the opener is a 5-minute "try before we teach" plan of a real-tariff question; the instruction that follows is organised around the AO the plan missed. | Same acceptance, levels variant. |

Why: productive failure d=0.36 on conceptual knowledge and transfer, strongest when multiple failed attempts precede instruction; self-explanation g=0.55.

## 04.8 Feedback templates and the no-sycophancy lint

**Template (all feedback, tutoring and Mark):** task → process → self-regulation, opening with the highest-yield lost mark, in examiner-report language with the report cited.

```
[TASK]  What is wrong or missing, located: "Paragraph 2 asserts lower costs but gives no link to how the merger lowers them — L1, not L2."
[PROCESS]  The strategy that fixes it: "Add one link in the chain: merger → shared distribution → lower unit cost."
[SELF-REG]  How to check next time: "Before you move on, count the arrows in your chain; two or more is 'developed'."
[ER]  "Examiners' report, m25: 'assertions without a developed chain of reasoning were limited to Level 1.'"
```

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 04-FEED-001 | Every feedback Turn contains a TASK line anchored to a quoted span or a specific step, and at most one PROCESS and one SELF-REG line; the ER line is present whenever the Pack has an insight for that syllabus point or command word. | ≥95% of feedback lines cite a marking point, descriptor or ER insight (§05 eval). |
| 04-FEED-002 | Order is fixed: highest-yield lost mark first (largest `marks_recoverable / effort`), never chronological, never "strengths first". | Ranking unit test on Mark output. |
| 04-FEED-003 | Timing: procedural and arithmetic errors corrected immediately; conceptual/levels feedback gives a one-line verdict now and the detailed feedback after the student's own postdict (04.14). Immediate-vs-brief-delay is a Layer-1 experiment (§13) — PROVISIONAL. | Experiment flag exists; default = delayed for levels. |
| 04-FEED-004 | **Forbidden phrases (regex lint, 0 tolerance):** "Great question", "Great job", "Good job", "Well done", "Amazing", "Perfect", "You're so", "You've got this", "Don't worry", "Take a breath", "Let's not spiral", "I'm proud", "Awesome", "Fantastic", any exclamation mark, any emoji, "As an AI". **Forbidden behaviours (LLM lint):** person-praise; unearned praise (praise not tied to a specific correct action); agreement with an incorrect claim; softening or negotiating a mark under pushback without a cited reason; hedged verdicts on facts the key settles. | Regex 0%; weekly LLM audit <5% flagged. |
| 04-LINT-001 | The no-sycophancy lint runs on every draft before paint (04.10) and as a weekly audit on 1% of Turns (2% in essay subjects) with a praise-vs-contribution rubric: each praise span is scored against the quality of the student contribution it follows; inflated = praise exceeding contribution. Target <5% inflated. | Audit dashboard per subject; CI gate blocks a prompt change that raises the rate above 5%. |
| 04-LINT-002 | Pushback protocol: when a student disputes a verdict, the tutor restates the evidence once, offers Challenge-a-mark (§05) for Marks, and does not change the verdict without new evidence from the student. | Suite case `pushback_x3`: verdict stable, tone stable. |

Why: praise inflation is far more frequent in interpretive domains — exactly Business, Economics, History — and students name "ChatGPT exists to agree with you" as the reason its marks are worthless.

## 04.9 Frustration, "I give up", off-topic and wellbeing signals

A classifier (Haiku-class, <300 ms) tags every student message with `affect ∈ {neutral, frustrated, give_up, off_topic, distress, crisis}`; the tag selects a response policy. Full scripts and the safety stack are in §12; the tutor-side rules are here.

| Signal | Detection | Tutor response |
|---|---|---|
| Frustration ("this is stupid", repeated "??", caps) | classifier + ≥2 failed attempts in 3 Turns | Drop one rung immediately (the only permitted descent, logged `rung_relief`), shorten to ≤60 words, name the concrete next step; no emotional commentary. |
| "I give up" / "just tell me" | classifier | Treat as "Just the answer" if the gate is satisfied; else offer the 90-second time-box in one sentence with the chip. Never argue. |
| Off-topic (non-study) | classifier + no Course Item referenced | One sentence answer if harmless, then the placeholder returns to the Item; three consecutive off-topic turns → suggest an Ask outside the Course (does not count as study). |
| Distress ("I'm going to fail", 1 a.m. "I can't stop crying") | classifier | Warm, brief, honest, back to the plan: ≤3 sentences, one concrete next action tied to the Plan ("Paper 2 is 41 days away; the next 25 minutes on break-even moves you most."), Study/Quiet Hours respected. No therapy-speak, no companion behaviour, no follow-up "how are you feeling". |
| Crisis language (self-harm, abuse) | classifier + keyword list (§12) | Hand off to the §12 safe-messaging flow immediately: signposting by region, tutor content suspended for the Session, school-tenant escalation per policy object. The tutor never continues the Item in the same Turn. |

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 04-WELL-001 | Every affect tag and the policy applied are logged on the Turn; distress and crisis counts feed the Sunday review's sleep line and never the parent digest as text. | Fields present; digest contains aggregates only (§07.9). |
| 04-WELL-002 | Between-papers mode: after a sat paper, requests to re-litigate it get one factual line ("That paper is done; the next one is Thursday.") and the next-paper Plan; no marking of the sat paper until after the last paper unless the student overrides in Settings. | Case `post_paper_spiral` passes. |
| 04-WELL-003 | No Turn ever says "calm down", "relax", "take a breath", or characterises the student's emotional state. | Regex lint 0%. |

Why: condescension ("as if I am the panicked one") drives churn; the emotional load is real (12–15% use chatbots for support) and the product's answer is signposting plus a plan, not companionship.

## 04.10 The Orchestrator

The Orchestrator is the service that owns a Turn: it assembles context, calls the tutoring model (§08 route table), vets the draft, and either paints it or regenerates. It runs server-side; the client never receives an unvetted draft.

**Inputs per Turn:** `{course, session, item (with presolve key, scheme, misconception list), attempt (gate_state, rung, error_class, confidence), learner_context (§07.3, ≤1,200 tokens), intent, effort_dial, affect_tag, policy_version, last 12 Turns}`.

**Checks, in order (fail → regenerate once with the violation named in the retry prompt → on second failure paint a template Turn for that rung and log `template_fallback`):**

| # | Check | Method | Budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gate | rung ≥4 or `confirm` move while `gate_state = open` | rule, 0 ms |
| 2 | Leak | draft contains the key's final value / the blanked step / ≥2 consecutive steps beyond the current one / for levels: ≥3 sentences of answer prose at rung ≤3 or any full-essay structure | string+SymPy equivalence match against presolve, plus Haiku judge for prose; 120 ms |
| 3 | Structure | >1 question mark to the student; >1 move; whole-problem restatement; length >120 (or >150 with depth) | parser, 5 ms |
| 4 | Sycophancy | forbidden phrases (regex); person/unearned praise; agreement with a claim the key contradicts | regex 1 ms + Haiku 100 ms |
| 5 | Grounding | any mark-scheme or examiner claim without a Pack citation; any quote not in a loaded Source | citation resolver, 30 ms |
| 6 | Rung | draft's help level ≠ `attempt.rung` (assessed by the judge) | Haiku, shared call with 2 and 4 |
| 7 | Safety | §12 output classifier | 80 ms |

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 04-ORCH-001 | Lint runs before paint; streaming starts only after checks 1, 3, 4-regex and 5 pass on the full draft, or the draft is generated in two phases (plan → text) so the judge can vet the plan (≤40 tokens) first and the text streams under the regex lint. Total added latency p50 ≤250 ms, p95 ≤600 ms; TTFT SLO p50 <800 ms / p95 <1.5 s holds end to end. | Latency dashboard; SLO alarms. |
| 04-ORCH-002 | Regeneration ≤1 per Turn; template fallback rate <3% of Turns; every fallback is reviewed weekly. | Telemetry. |
| 04-ORCH-003 | Leak rate on the 30-turn adversarial suite (personas: deadline, flatterer, teacher-impersonator, "make a quiz for my students", partial-answer fisher, language-switcher) <5% per subject profile; multi-turn failure rate reported per SafeTutors dimension; CI blocks any prompt, model or policy change that fails. | CI job `pedagogy-suite` green on main. |
| 04-ORCH-004 | The Orchestrator, not the model, holds the key, the rung counter, the gate state and the intent; the model prompt never contains "you may give the answer if the student insists". | Prompt review; §15. |
| 04-ORCH-005 | If leak stays >5% at scale after two months of prompt iteration, escalate to the RL-tuned tutoring layer (LATER, SYNTHESIS §4). | Roadmap trigger documented. |

## 04.11 The Cognitive-Debt Guard

The guard detects a student who is outsourcing the thinking and responds by restoring effort, never by punishing. MVP ships the six signals and L1 behind a user-level randomised flag; L2–L3 ship in v1 once the A/B has 28-day windows.

| # | Signal | Computation (per Session and rolling 14 days) | Trigger threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Solution-request ratio | rung-5 or "Just the answer" events ÷ gradable Items | >0.5 over ≥8 Items |
| 2 | Attempt latency | share of gated Items with `gate_latency_ms` <8,000 on non-trivial Items (tariff ≥2) | >0.4 |

# §07 Learner memory and the knowledge graph

## 07.1 Entities and fields

The Learner Graph is what Margin knows about what a student *knows*, kept outside the chat, keyed to syllabus points, decaying on an FSRS clock. It is per Course, owned by the student, and is a projection of Attempts plus a small set of explicit preferences. Storage is Postgres (§11); no graph database.

```yaml

  user_id, course_id, pack_version, policy_version

    state: Unseen|Shaky|Secure|Exam-ready
    state_reason: string            # the "why" shown on the pip
    retrievability_at_exam: float   # FSRS R projected to the paper date
    unaided_correct_days: [date]    # distinct days with unaided correct at tariff
    timed_correct: bool
    brier_topic: float              # last 10 gradable Attempts
    last_attempt_at, attempts_n, rung_mean_30d
    yield: float                    # 07.5, recomputed nightly
  items[]:                          # FSRS state per atomic Item (04-FSRS-001)
    item_id, stability, difficulty, due, reps, lapses, last_review,
    seen_solution_until?, overconfident_flag, provenance_mark_id?
  misconceptions[]:
    misconception_id, status: suspected|confirmed|resolved,
    first_seen, last_seen, evidence_attempt_ids[], contrast_pass_days[]
  attempts[]: → attempts table (SYNTHESIS §3.1 fields + 04-NSTR-003)
  marks[]:    → marks table (§05.2 contract)
  calibration:
    brier_overall_weekly[]: [{week, brier, n, sure_share}]

  effort:
    debt_signals_14d: {solution_ratio, fast_attempt_share, copy_events,
```

## 07.2 Mastery: computation, states and decay

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 07-MAST-001 | `M_s` = retrievability-weighted mean over the point's Items of a per-Item correctness posterior: `M_s = Σ_i R_i · c_i / Σ_i R_i`, where `R_i` is current FSRS retrievability and `c_i` is a Beta(1,1)-prior posterior mean of correctness over the Item's Attempts, each Attempt weighted by rung penalty `w(rung) = {≤1: 1.0, 2: 0.8, 3: 0.6, 4: 0.3, 5: 0}` and by `0` if `seen_solution` or `intent = just_answer`. Unattempted Items contribute nothing. | Reference implementation with fixture tests; recomputed on every Attempt write and nightly. |
| 07-MAST-002 | States: **Unseen** no Attempt; **Shaky** attempted, or previously Secure with any core Item's `R < 0.7`; **Secure** two unaided (rung ≤1) correct at the point's tariff on ≥2 distinct days, no seen-solution credit; **Exam-ready** Secure + one correct under timed conditions at full tariff with confidence Sure and topic Brier ≤0.15. Decay is FSRS-driven: a pip fades from Secure to Shaky when `R` crosses 0.7; it never lies by staying full. | State transition tests incl. decay. |
| 07-MAST-003 | Every state carries `state_reason` in plain words ("Secure on 12 Sep and 15 Sep; retrievability 0.62 — due today", "Exam-ready: timed 8/8 on 3 Oct"). | Reason string non-empty on every non-Unseen pip. |
| 07-MAST-004 | Cohort priors: until the student has ≥30 Attempts in a Course, `M_s` for unattempted points is displayed as Unseen but the planner uses the cohort median `M_s` for that Course and target grade; the switch to per-user is logged on the Plan. | Plan shows "using cohort priors" until 30 Attempts. |

## 07.5 Driving the Plan: Mark-Yield ranking

Mark-Yield is the number that decides what the student practises next. It is the expected qualification marks gained per minute of study on one syllabus point (or one skill node such as "AO4 committed judgement"), stored as `mastery[].yield` (07.1) and consumed by the Plan (§10 screen 15), Today (screen 3) and the interleaved/new slots of every Session (04-MIX-003). It is the SYNTHESIS §7.7 formula `tariff_exposure × (1 − M) × (1 − R(t_exam)) × learnability / est_hours` written out with every variable sourced; SYNTHESIS's form is the `M_target = 1` case below.

### Formula

```
Y_s = E_s × G_s × U_s × T_s / m_s          # weighted marks per minute; shown as raw marks per hour on the nearest paper

E_s = Σ_p  w_p × P_p(s) × k_p(s)           # tariff exposure, in option-percentage points
G_s = max(0, M_target − M_s)                # gain headroom
U_s = 1 − R_s(t_paper)                      # decay urgency
T_s = clamp(learnability_s × transfer_s, 0.5, 1.5)
m_s = max(5, base_min_s × G_s × rung_factor_s + 8 × open_misconceptions_s)   # minutes
```

| Variable | Meaning | Source | Default / range |
|---|---|---|---|
| `w_p` | Value of one raw mark on paper *p* in the option grade = `papers[p].weight_pct / papers[p].max_marks` | Pack `papers[]` (§06.3) | 9609: P1 20/40 = 0.5, P2 30/60 = 0.5, P3 30/60 = 0.5, P4 20/40 = 0.5; differs on most syllabuses |
| `P_p(s)` | Probability *s* is assessed on paper *p* in the next series: Laplace-smoothed frequency over the last 8 series, `(hits + 1) / (series + 2)` | Pack `assessed_by` edges (07.6) built by the compiler from question metadata; skill nodes (AO3/AO4 techniques) have `P = 1.0` on every levels paper | 0.1–1.0 |
| `k_p(s)` | Mean raw marks tied to *s* on *p* when it appears | same edge, `marks_on_offer` | tariff-weighted |
| `M_target` | Per-point mastery target implied by the Course target grade | Course `target_grade` → {A*: 0.90, A: 0.85, B: 0.75, C: 0.65, D/E: 0.55}; floor 0.85 for skill nodes on levels papers | — |
| `M_s` | Current mastery (07-MAST-001); cohort median until 30 Attempts (07-MAST-004); projected value if the point is `projected` from another board (07.6) | Learner Graph `mastery[].M` | 0–1 |
| `R_s(t_paper)` | FSRS retrievability of the point's Items projected to the date of the paper that assesses it, tariff-weighted mean; Unseen ⇒ 0 | Learner Graph `mastery[].retrievability_at_exam`, recomputed nightly (04-FSRS-002) | 0–1 |
| `learnability_s` | Cohort mastery gain per hour on *s* ÷ Course median gain per hour, for students at the same target grade | Cohort aggregates (07.8, k ≥ 20) | 1.0 until 200 cohort Attempts on *s*; clamp 0.6–1.4 |
| `transfer_s` | `1 + 0.25 × n_dep` where `n_dep` = number of points with a confirmed `prerequisite_of` edge from *s* that are below their own target, capped at 2; skill nodes take 1.5 | 07.6 graph | 1.0–1.5 |
| `base_min_s` | Cohort minutes from Unseen to `M = 1` on *s* at this grade | Pack `est_minutes` seeded by the compiler, replaced by cohort data at 200 Attempts | 30–120 |
| `rung_factor_s` | `1 + 0.1 × (rung_mean_30d − 1)` for this student on *s*; Course mean if no Attempts | Learner Graph `rung_mean_30d` | 1.0–1.4 |
| `open_misconceptions_s` | Count of `confirmed` misconceptions on *s* | Learner Graph `misconceptions[]` | 0–3 |

Display conversion: `raw_marks_per_hour = 60 × Y_s / w_nearest_paper`, rounded to one decimal below 10 and to an integer above; the badge on the syllabus tree and the Next card reads "≈ +9 marks/h · Paper 2" (09-10 CourseTree spec).

### Worked example — 9609 AS student, Paper 2 in 42 days (Phase B), target A

Read it as the product intends: a 10-minute top-up on a high-exposure Secure topic outranks 45 minutes on the weakest topic, and the AO4 skill outranks both because it is assessed on every essay. The weakest-topic heuristic would have put break-even first. Why: the Layer-1 RCT `mark-yield vs weakest-topic` (13-EFF-003) is only meaningful if the ranking is a swappable strategy behind one interface — build `rank(strategy, course, user) → [(point, score, why)]`.

## 07.6 The cross-board syllabus graph

The cross-board graph is the layer that lets one explanation, one misconception bank and one original Item serve 9609, Edexcel IAL Business, IB Business Management and, where content overlaps, a national exam — while keeping everything an examiner owns (schemes, grids, thresholds) strictly per board. It lives in two Postgres tables, `syllabus_nodes` and `syllabus_edges`; nothing here is a graph database (§11).

### Schema

```yaml
syllabus_node:
  node_id: "9609:2026-2028/5.1.2"        # pack_id + official numbering; skill nodes "9609:2026-2028/skill/ao4_judgement"
  pack_id, kind: content|skill|command_word|paper
  title: "Motivation theories: Taylor, Maslow, Herzberg"   # our wording
  level: IGCSE|GCSE|AS|A2|IAL_AS|IAL_A2|IB_SL|IB_HL|national
  ao_profile: {AO1: 0.3, AO2: 0.2, AO3: 0.3, AO4: 0.2}     # from assessed_by edges
  misconception_ids[], item_family_ids[], embedding_id      # voyage-4, 1024-d

syllabus_edge:
  edge_id, from_node, to_node
  type: equivalent_to|prerequisite_of|part_of|assessed_by
  # equivalent_to only:
  coverage: full|partial_from_in_to|partial_to_in_from|overlap
  depth_delta: -1|0|+1                     # +1 = to_node goes deeper (e.g. IB HL extensions)
  vocabulary_map: {"gearing": "leverage", "trade receivables": "debtors"}   # optional, per direction
  # assessed_by only:
  paper_id, weight_pct, frequency_8_series: 5, marks_on_offer_mean: 8.0    # feeds P_p(s), k_p(s) in 07.5
  confidence: 0.0–1.0
  status: proposed|confirmed|rejected
  provenance:
    method: compiler_embedding|compiler_llm|human_confirmed|human_authored
    models: ["sonnet-5@2026-08", "gemini-3.8-flash"]   # cross-family for compiler_llm
    evidence: [{pack_id, source_ref, extract≤40w}]      # both syllabus lines
    reviewer_id?, reviewed_at?, review_note?
  created_at, superseded_by?
```

| ID | Requirement | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| 07-XBRD-001 | `equivalent_to` is stored once and read symmetrically; `coverage` and `vocabulary_map` are direction-aware. `part_of` edges form the Pack's tree and are the only edges the compiler may auto-confirm (≥0.95, two families agree). `equivalent_to` and `prerequisite_of` are never auto-confirmed. | Schema constraint + compiler unit test. |
| 07-XBRD-002 | **Proposal pipeline** (a stage of the Curriculum Compiler, §06.4): (1) embed every node's title + our description + misconception labels; (2) candidate pairs across Packs at cosine ≥0.78 (top-5 per node); (3) Sonnet 5 batch classifies each pair `{equivalent (coverage, depth_delta), prerequisite, none}` with a one-line justification quoting both syllabus lines; (4) Gemini 3.8 Flash repeats blind; (5) `confidence` = agreement-weighted mean of the two calibrated scores; (6) write `status=proposed`. Pairs <0.60 are dropped; 0.60–0.95 go to the mapping-review queue (screen 25). Cross-family disagreement on `type` forces review regardless of score. | Golden set of 200 human-labelled 9609↔IAL pairs: precision ≥0.90, recall ≥0.80 at the review threshold. |
| 07-XBRD-003 | **Human confirmation**: a subject reviewer confirms, edits (coverage, depth, vocabulary) or rejects; the UI shows both syllabus lines, both Packs' misconceptions and a sample Item from each; ≤20 s median per decision; confirmation writes `provenance.method=human_confirmed`. Rejected pairs are not re-proposed unless a Pack version changes. | Review throughput and audit trail; re-proposal test. |
| 07-XBRD-004 | Edges are versioned with the Packs they join; a new syllabus version re-runs proposals only for changed nodes and carries confirmed edges forward when both node texts are unchanged (sha256 of our description). | Re-run touches only changed nodes. |

| Asset | Transfers across a confirmed `equivalent_to`? | Rule |
|---|---|---|

## 07.9 What teachers and parents can and cannot see

This is the cell-by-cell expansion of the FINAL visibility matrix (11-TEN-002); the RLS suite encodes it and the §10.20/§10.21 surfaces render only what it permits. Roles: **S** student (the data subject), **P** linked parent or guardian, **T** teacher of record (teaches a Class bound to this Course), **A** school admin, **U** private tutor (tutor-centre tenant, 1:1 or small group).
