## 30 seconds

Evaluation is not a summary and it is not balance. It is a judgement: naming which factor decides the answer, saying why it outweighs the strongest counter-argument, and attaching the condition under which you would change your mind.

## Standard

Generic conclusions score close to nothing. "In conclusion, it depends on the situation" contains no judgement, no reasoning and nothing specific to the case, so there is nothing for a marker to credit.

Deploy two or three of the eight named moves per answer, always tied to case specifics. Two well-used moves beat eight mentioned ones.

## Exam level

The four-sentence conclusion template reaches the top band reliably:

> On balance, [firm] should [decision], because [the decisive factor] — which in this case outweighs [the strongest counter-argument] since [case-specific reason]. This holds provided [condition drawn from the case]; if [condition fails], [alternative] becomes preferable.

Four sentences. It commits, it justifies, it weighs, and it conditions — which is, almost exactly, the top-band descriptor.

Two structural notes that cost real marks:

**Front-load judgement on "Evaluate" and "Assess".** Saving it all for the final paragraph risks running out of time before you write the marks-bearing part of the answer.

**Do not evaluate an "Analyse" question.** There are no AO4 marks available. The judgement earns nothing and the minutes come out of a question where they would have.

**Most commonly lost here:** writing a balanced answer with no decision in it, and calling the summary a conclusion.

## Misconceptions

- Evaluation means giving both sides → Both sides is analysis. Evaluation is choosing between them and saying why.
- A conclusion should summarise the essay → A conclusion should answer the question. The marker has just read your points.
- Hedging is safer than committing → Hedging is where AO4 marks go to die. A defensible wrong judgement outscores no judgement.
