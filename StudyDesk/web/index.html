Two ideas hold the whole thing up

Most "AI in a PDF" apps take a screenshot and hope. Study Desk doesn't need to, because of how the page is built.

01 The context engine

The printed question comes from the PDF's own text layer. Your working comes from reading the Apple Pencil ink layer — on your iPad, on its own, with nothing printed in the picture.

Because those are physically different layers, the tutor can be told the question is 2x + 5 = 15 and the student answered x = 4, rather than being handed an image and asked to guess. That is what makes "check my work" and "find my mistake" produce real feedback instead of a description of the page.

02 The Pencil engine

Ink is not painted over a picture of the page. Every PDF page gets a real PencilKit canvas positioned by PDFKit itself, so PDFKit owns zoom and scrolling and PencilKit owns the strokes.

No coordinate maths runs while your hand is moving — which is exactly what makes ink drift away from the page in apps that do it the other way. Strokes stay vector, stay sharp at any zoom, and belong to a page, so they survive rotation, Split View and a different iPad.

Ask about exactly what's confusing you

The tutor button reads the situation before you tap it.

  • Nothing selected → Ask about this page
  • Text selected → Explain this
  • You've written something → Check my work
  • A question detected → Help with this question
  • A box dragged round a diagram → Ask about this part

Hints before answers — but never a locked door

Ask for the answer and the tutor offers a hint first. Both buttons are equally easy to press. The point is to make the smaller step visible, not to make the bigger one hard.

Four verdicts, not two

Checking your work returns correct, nearly there, not yet, or can't quite read it. That last one matters: a student marked wrong because of their handwriting stops showing anyone their working.

Writing beats everything else

If a feature ever conflicts with Pencil latency, the feature loses. Some consequences of taking that seriously:

Fingers scroll, Pencil writes

Palm rejection and two-finger pan come from PencilKit's own input policy, not from a gesture recogniser fighting it.

Saves in under a second

Ink updates in memory instantly and is written at most once a second — and immediately when you change page or leave the app.

Your original is never touched

The imported PDF is stored byte-for-byte and never written to. Your work is a separate layer; the export is a third file.

Sharp at any zoom

Canvases re-render at the zoomed resolution, so ink laid down at 100% is still crisp when you zoom in to check a fraction.

500 pages stays smooth

Drawings are held only for the pages around you. A textbook doesn't load 500 canvases to show you page 12.

Handwriting stays yours

Anything the tutor suggests drawing is a distinct colour, clearly marked, and never applied without you saying so.

Finishing is part of the app

"It's in the app" and "my teacher has it" are not the same thing. Everything between them is where marks get lost.

  1. Final reviewBlank pages flagged, work confirmed saved, page count shown.
  2. A real PDFThe original page drawn as vector — text stays selectable — with your ink on top at high resolution.
  3. You see it firstFile name, page count, size. Nothing is sent until you've looked.
  4. Sent your wayThe iPad share sheet, so it reaches Teams, Classroom, email or whatever your school uses.
  5. KeptThe exact file you sent stays in Submitted, so "what did I hand in?" always has an answer.

What actually leaves the iPad

School documents contain real things about real children. This is the whole list, and the app shows it to you at the moment you ask a question.

Stays on the iPad

  • Your PDFs, in full
  • Your handwriting, as ink
  • Handwriting recognition — it runs on the device
  • Your notes and your study history
  • Every page you didn't ask about

Sent, only when you ask a question

  • The printed text of that one page
  • A short text reading of your handwriting
  • One picture of that page, or the part you selected — switchable off
  • Your last few messages in that chat

No account. No email. No advertising identifier. The app holds one anonymous token so the server can rate-limit an install, and reinstalling replaces it. Turn page pictures off and everything works as text — diagrams stop, the rest doesn't.

The AI and voice keys are never on the iPad. Requests go through a small proxy that holds them, because a key shipped inside an app is a key that has been given away, however well it is hidden.
