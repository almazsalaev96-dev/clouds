# Cambridge International AS & A Level Business (9609) — A/A* Master Plan

> **Syllabus code correction:** 9708 is **Economics**. Business is **9609**.
> This plan targets the **2026–2028 syllabus** (the one live for your exams).

---

## PART 1 — THE MARK ARITHMETIC (read this first, it changes everything)

### The four papers

| Paper | Name | Time | Raw marks | % of A Level | Format |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Business Concepts 1 (AS) | 1h 15m | 40 | 20% | Section A: 4 compulsory short answers (5 marks each). Section B: 1 essay from a choice of 2 (20 marks) |
| 2 | Business Concepts 2 (AS) | 1h 30m | 60 | 30% | 2 compulsory data-response questions on a case-study insert |
| 3 | Business Decision-Making (A2) | 1h 45m | 60 | 30% | Case study + data; decision-focused |
| 4 | Business Strategy (A2) | 1h 15m | 40 | 20% | 2 compulsory 20-mark essays on a case study |

**A Level total = 200 raw marks.** Weighting = raw marks ÷ 2 for every paper.
→ **Every raw mark is worth exactly the same, on every paper.** No paper deserves
disproportionate panic. Marks are marks.

**AS Level only** = Papers 1 + 2 → P1 is 40% of AS, P2 is 60% of AS.

### The assessment objectives

| AO | What it is |
|---|---|
| **AO1 Knowledge & understanding** | Demonstrate knowledge of business concepts, terms and theories |
| **AO2 Application** | Apply that knowledge to problems in **specific, named business contexts** |
| **AO3 Analysis** | Analyse using appropriate methods; work with qualitative and quantitative information; find causes, impacts and consequences; separate fact from opinion; draw valid inferences |
| **AO4 Evaluation** | Evaluate evidence to make **reasoned judgements**, substantiated conclusions and recommendations |

### AO weightings — the single most important table in this document

| | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | **A Level total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AO1 Knowledge | 35% | 30% | 20% | 15% | **25%** |
| AO2 Application | 30% | 30% | 27% | 10% | **25%** |
| AO3 Analysis | 20% | 20% | 23% | 40% | **25%** |
| AO4 Evaluation | 15% | 20% | 30% | 35% | **25%** |

Converted into **actual raw marks** out of 200:

| AO | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | **Total marks** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AO1 | 14 | 18 | 12 | 6 | **50** |
| AO2 | 12 | 18 | 16 | 4 | **50** |
| AO3 | 8 | 12 | 14 | 16 | **50** |
| AO4 | 6 | 12 | 18 | 14 | **50** |

*(Cross-checks exactly: 50 marks per AO, 200 total.)*

### The three conclusions that decide your grade

**1. Only 50 of 200 marks (25%) reward knowing things.**
AO1 is the smallest quarter, and it's the one almost every student spends
80% of their revision time on. Memorising the syllabus, perfectly, caps you at
about a C/B. This is the number-one reason capable students miss A*.

**2. 100 of 200 marks (AO3 + AO4) are *skills*, not content.**
They are trained by writing and self-marking, not by reading. They transfer
across topics — an evaluation technique learned on marketing works on finance.
This is the highest-return revision that exists in this subject.

**3. Paper 4 is the densest A* lever in the qualification.**
75% of Paper 4 is AO3+AO4 (40% analysis, 35% evaluation). It is only 40 marks
and only two essays. Two well-built essays = 30 skill marks. Nothing else in
the syllabus pays that rate per hour of preparation.

### Derived essay mark split (Paper 4, per 20-mark essay)

The published weightings force this split arithmetically:

| AO1 | AO2 | AO3 | AO4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 marks | 2 marks | 8 marks | 7 marks |

**Read that again.** Knowledge + application = 5 marks. Analysis + evaluation = 15.
A beautifully defined, perfectly contextualised essay with no chains of reasoning
and no judgement scores **5/20**. Examiner reports say exactly this: candidates
"develop only limited evaluative sections."

*(Paper 1's Section B essay is more knowledge-weighted — roughly AO1 4 / AO2 4 /
AO3 6 / AO4 6. Treat that as approximate; the P4 split above is exact.)*

### Time discipline — minutes per mark

| Paper | Budget | Practical plan |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | 1.9 min/mark | 35 min Section A (~8 min per 5-marker) · 5 min essay plan · 33 min essay |
| P2 | 1.5 min/mark | 10 min reading the case · 80 min answering (1.33 min/mark) |
| P3 | 1.75 min/mark | 15 min reading + data · 90 min answering |
| P4 | 1.9 min/mark | 15 min case + planning both essays · 30 min each essay |

**Rule:** when a question's time is up, stop mid-sentence and move on.
The last 5 marks of an over-run question are the hardest marks on the paper;
the first 5 marks of the next question are the easiest. Abandoning on time
is worth more than any content you know.

## PART 2 — WHAT EXAMINERS ACTUALLY REWARD

Drawn from CAIE 9609 examiner reports and example-candidate-response commentary.

### AO2 Application — the fastest 50 marks to fix

**Naming the business is not application.** Writing "This would help Zeta Ltd
increase sales" earns nothing. The examiner reports are explicit: repeating the
case-study name without specific reference does not count.

Application means using something **only true of this business**:
- a number from the case ("with a gearing ratio of 68%...")
- its market ("in a price-elastic, low-margin commodity market...")
- its stated objective ("given the owner's stated aim to sell within 3 years...")
- its constraint ("with only $40 000 of retained profit available...")
- its stage/size/ownership ("as a family-owned business with no access to share capital...")

**Test:** cover the business name. If the sentence still works for any random
company, it is not application. Rewrite it.

### AO3 Analysis — build chains, not lists

A "point" is worth nothing. A **chain** is worth marks. Minimum three links:

> Cause → mechanism → effect → **consequence for this business**

Example (weak): *"Delegation improves motivation."* → 1 mark at best.

Example (strong): *"Delegating stock ordering to branch managers gives them
control over a decision that visibly affects their branch's performance, which
satisfies the esteem needs Maslow places above security — and because Bela's
staff turnover of 34% is already concentrated in supervisory grades, retaining
those managers directly reduces the $18 000 recruitment cost the case reports."*
→ full chain, contextual, quantified.

**Analysis vocabulary that signals a chain:** *which means · so · therefore ·
as a result · this leads to · consequently · which in turn.*
If a paragraph contains none of these words, it contains no analysis.

### AO4 Evaluation — the eight moves

This is where A* is won and lost. Generic conclusions ("in conclusion, it depends
on the situation") score near zero. Use these **eight named moves** — deploy 2–3
per essay, always tied to case specifics:

1. **Depends-on** — name the variable the whole answer hinges on
   *("This holds only if demand is price-inelastic; the case's 15% sales drop
   after a 5% price rise suggests it isn't.")*
2. **Short run vs long run** — a decision that hurts now and pays later, or vice versa
3. **Magnitude / relative significance** — of the factors discussed, **which matters
   most and why**. Ranking is evaluation; listing is not.
4. **Stakeholder conflict** — who gains, who loses, whose claim is stronger *here*
5. **Quality of the evidence** — sample size, age of data, opinion presented as fact,
   forecast assumptions. (Explicitly named in AO3 too — high-value.)
6. **Opportunity cost / the alternative** — what else could that money or time do?
7. **Risk and reversibility** — how bad if it fails, and can it be undone?
8. **Assumption challenge** — the theory assumes X; in this business X does not hold

### The conclusion that gets full AO4

A full-mark conclusion is **not** a summary. It must:
- give a **clear, committed answer** (yes/no/which one — never "both have merits")
- state **why this factor outweighed the others** (that's the judgement)
- attach a **condition or caveat** drawn from the case
- where the command word says *recommend*, say **what to do, and when**

Template:

> "On balance, [firm] should [decision], because [the decisive factor] —
> which in this case outweighs [the strongest counter-argument] since [case-specific
> reason]. This holds provided [condition from the case]; if [condition fails],
> [alternative] becomes preferable."

Four sentences. Reliably worth the top AO4 band.

### Command words → AO ceiling

| Command word | Highest AO available | What that means practically |
|---|---|---|
| Define / State / Identify | AO1 | One precise sentence. **No explanation needed** — examiners note explanation here wastes time |
| Explain / Briefly explain | AO1+AO2 | Definition + how it applies here. No judgement |
| Calculate | AO1+AO2 | Show working, state units, **convert to % where asked** |
| Analyse | AO1–AO3 | Chains. **Do NOT evaluate — there are no AO4 marks to earn** |
| Discuss | AO1–AO4 | Both sides, then judge |
| Evaluate / Assess | AO1–AO4 | Judgement dominant — front-load evaluation |
| Recommend / Advise | AO1–AO4 | Must end with a specific, justified course of action |

**Highest-frequency avoidable error:** writing evaluation into "Analyse" questions
(zero marks, real minutes lost) and writing no evaluation into "Evaluate" questions.

### Calculation traps flagged by examiners

- **Inverting the formula** — profit ÷ revenue, *not* revenue ÷ profit
- **Forgetting to × 100** — a ratio is not a percentage
- **Mark-up ≠ margin** — a 50% mark-up on cost is a 33.3% profit margin
- Always **state the unit** ($, %, days, times)
- Always **write one sentence interpreting** the number — the mark is often for
  what it *means*, not the arithmetic

### Presentation

- **One paragraph per point.** Examiner reports state directly that separate
  paragraphs greatly increase the chance each point is recognised. Wall-of-text
  answers lose marks that were actually earned.
- Label nothing, but structure everything.

## PART 3 — THE SYLLABUS MAP

AS Level = sections **1–5**. A Level = sections **1–10** (6–10 extend 1–5 strategically).

### AS Level

**1. Business and its environment** — 1.1 Enterprise · 1.2 Business structure ·
1.3 Size of business · 1.4 Business objectives · 1.5 Stakeholders

**2. People in organisations (HRM)** — 2.1 Human resource management ·
2.2 Motivation · 2.3 Management

**3. Marketing** — 3.1 The nature of marketing · 3.2 Market research ·
3.3 The marketing mix

**4. Operations management** — 4.1 The nature of operations · inventory ·
capacity · quality

**5. Finance and accounting** — sources of finance · costs · break-even ·
budgets · cash flow · financial statements

### A Level extension

**6. Business and its environment** — external influences (political, legal,
economic, social, technological, competitive, international, environmental) ·
business strategy · corporate planning

**7. People in organisations** — 7.1 Organisational structure · 7.2 Business
communication · 7.3 Leadership · 7.4 HRM strategy

**8. Marketing** — elasticity · product development · sales forecasting ·
marketing planning · international marketing

**9. Operations and project management** — location and scale · quality
management · lean production · ERP · **critical path analysis**

**10. Finance and accounting** — financial statements and **ratio analysis** ·
**investment appraisal** · long-term financing strategy

### Numerical toolkit (make these automatic — they are free AO1/AO2 marks)

**AS:** market share · market growth · revenue · fixed/variable/total cost ·
contribution per unit · break-even output · margin of safety · gross, operating
and profit margins · ROCE · current ratio · acid-test ratio · labour productivity
· labour turnover · absenteeism rate · capacity utilisation · added value ·
price / income / promotional elasticity of demand · cash-flow forecast · working capital

**A2:** gearing · inventory turnover · trade receivable days · dividend yield ·
dividend cover · earnings per share · price/earnings ratio · **payback ·
average rate of return (ARR) · net present value (NPV) · internal rate of return
(IRR)** · decision trees (expected values) · **critical path analysis** (EST, LFT,
float) · moving averages and time-series forecasting · correlation · economic
order quantity · re-order level

**Frameworks to have on instant recall:** SWOT · PEST(LE) · Ansoff matrix ·
Boston (BCG) matrix · Porter's five forces · Porter's generic strategies ·
Force-field analysis · Maslow · Herzberg · Taylor · Mayo · McGregor X/Y ·
Kaizen / lean / JIT · Handy and Mintzberg on structure

## PART 4 — THE LEARNING SCIENCE (how to actually make it stick)

Everything here is drawn from replicated findings in cognitive psychology, and
each is mapped to a concrete 9609 action. Method matters more than hours.

### The core four

**1. Retrieval practice (the testing effect).**
Recalling information strengthens memory far more than re-reading it
(Roediger & Karpicke). Closing the book and writing what you know beats
highlighting by a wide margin.
→ *9609 action:* never read a topic without immediately closing the page and
writing the whole thing from blank paper. Then compare and fill gaps in red.

**2. Spaced repetition.**
Material revisited at expanding intervals is retained far longer than
material studied in one block.
→ *9609 action:* every topic gets touched on **day 1, day 3, day 7, day 16, day 35**.
Each touch is a 10-minute blank-paper recall, not a re-read.

**3. Interleaving.**
Mixing topics beats blocking them. It feels harder and worse — and produces
better exam performance, because the exam itself interleaves.
→ *9609 action:* never revise one section for a whole session. Mix finance +
motivation + marketing in a single sitting. Papers 2, 3 and 4 jump across the
syllabus within one case study; train for that.

**4. The fluency illusion — your main enemy.**
Highlighting, re-reading and watching videos create a *feeling* of mastery
that does not survive an exam hall. Fluency of recognition is mistaken for
ability to produce.
→ *9609 action:* the only valid evidence you know something is **closed-book
output that you then mark against a real mark scheme**. Ban highlighters.

### The amplifiers

**5. Elaborative interrogation.** For every concept, ask "*why* does this work,
and *when* does it fail?" The "when it fails" half is literally your AO4 bank.

**6. The generation effect.** Attempt the answer *before* looking at the mark
scheme, even when you're sure you'll fail. Struggling first makes the eventual
answer stick far harder than reading it cold.

**7. Pre-testing.** Attempt questions on topics you have *not yet studied*.
The failed attempt primes you and makes the subsequent learning stick better.

**8. Delayed feedback.** Mark your own paper **the next day**, not immediately.
Delayed feedback produces better long-term retention than instant correction.

**9. Dual coding.** Pair every framework with a drawn diagram. Draw the Ansoff
matrix, the break-even chart, the network diagram — by hand, from memory.

**10. Deliberate practice.** Don't "revise Business." Target the single weakest
sub-skill you have evidence for. If your AO4 is weak, spend a week writing
*only conclusions* — twenty conclusions, no essays. Isolate, drill, reintegrate.

### Exam-day psychology (real effects, worth real marks)

- **Expressive writing.** Ten minutes of writing about your exam worries shortly
  before the exam has been shown to significantly raise scores in anxious
  students (Ramirez & Beilock, *Science*, 2011). It offloads the working memory
  that anxiety is consuming. Do it in the corridor.
- **Stress reappraisal.** Relabel arousal as fuel: "my heart is racing because
  my body is getting ready" outperforms trying to calm down. Suppression costs
  working memory; reappraisal doesn't.
- **Yerkes–Dodson.** Moderate arousal is optimal — neither flat nor panicked.
  For complex tasks like a 20-mark essay, the optimum is *lower* than you think.
- **Implementation intentions (Gollwitzer).** Pre-decide with if–then rules:
  *"If I don't know the answer, then I write the definition and move on."*
  *"If 30 minutes have passed on essay 1, then I start essay 2 regardless."*
  Pre-committed rules survive panic; good intentions don't.
- **Sleep.** Consolidation happens during sleep. A 5-hour night before the exam
  costs more marks than the 5 hours of cramming gain. This is not motivational
  advice — it's the mechanism by which yesterday's revision becomes memory.
- **The first five minutes.** Read the whole paper before writing. Start with the
  question you can answer best to establish momentum and settle arousal.

## PART 5 — THE PLAN

Phase lengths scale to whatever time you have. Percentages of remaining time
are given so it works whether you have 3 weeks or 9 months.

### Phase 0 — Diagnostic (one sitting, do this first)

Sit **one full past paper, cold, timed, closed-book** — before revising anything.
Mark it yourself against the official mark scheme, and tally your marks *by AO*.

You now know whether your problem is AO1 (content gaps), AO2 (generic writing),
AO3 (no chains) or AO4 (no judgement). **Everything after this is targeted at
your actual gap.** Almost everyone discovers it's AO3/AO4 — and almost everyone
would otherwise have spent the term fixing AO1.

### Phase 1 — Content spine (~25% of remaining time)

One page per syllabus topic. Handwritten. Each page carries exactly:
definition · the mechanism · one framework/formula · **two evaluation lines
("this fails when…")** · one real-world example.

Non-negotiable: each page is written **from memory first**, then corrected
against the textbook in a different colour. The correction pass *is* the learning.

### Phase 2 — Skill drilling (~35% of remaining time) ← the A* phase

This is separated from content deliberately, because AO3/AO4 are skills.

- **Chain drills:** take any concept, write a 3-link causal chain into a given
  context. Twenty a week. Five minutes each.
- **Evaluation-move drills:** take one past-paper essay title and write **only
  the conclusion**, using the four-sentence template. Ten a week.
- **Application drills:** take a generic sentence and rewrite it three ways for
  three different businesses from past-paper case studies.
- **Calculation sets:** every formula in the toolkit, until automatic — including
  the interpreting sentence after each answer.

### Phase 3 — Timed papers + self-marking (~30% of remaining time)

- Full papers under real time, no notes, no pausing.
- **Mark them yourself, next day, against official mark schemes.**
  Self-marking against a mark scheme is the single highest-value activity in
  A Level Business — you learn the marker's model of a good answer, which is
  the actual object of assessment.
- Keep an **error log**: every lost mark categorised by AO and by cause
  (didn't know it / didn't apply it / no chain / no judgement / ran out of time).
  Re-read the log before the next paper. Patterns appear within three papers.
- Work **backwards through recent series**, then across all variants (11/12/13,
  21/22/23 etc.) — variants are a large, underused bank of practice.

### Phase 4 — Taper (final ~10%)

- No new content. Spaced recall of the one-page spine only.
- One paper every other day, not every day.
- Rehearse the eight evaluation moves and the conclusion template until reflexive.
- Sleep, timing rules, implementation intentions.

### Weekly rhythm (adapt the number of blocks to your available time)

| | Focus |
|---|---|
| Mon | New/weak content + blank-paper recall |
| Tue | Chain drills (AO3) + calculation set |
| Wed | Timed section of a paper |
| Thu | Self-mark Wednesday's work + error log |
| Fri | Evaluation drills (AO4) — conclusions only |
| Sat | Full timed paper |
| Sun | Spaced recall of everything from days 3, 7, 16, 35 · rest |

## PART 6 — THE A* CHECKLIST

Before every essay you write, from now until the exam:

- [ ] Have I identified the **command word** and its AO ceiling?
- [ ] Is every point in its **own paragraph**?
- [ ] Does every paragraph contain *which means / so / therefore* — a **chain**, not a list?
- [ ] Is there something in every paragraph that is **only true of this business** —
      a number, a constraint, an objective, a market condition?
- [ ] Have I used **2–3 named evaluation moves**?
- [ ] Does my conclusion **commit to an answer**, say **why that factor outweighed
      the others**, and attach a **condition**?
- [ ] Did I finish **on time**?

Seven boxes. They are, almost exactly, the mark scheme.
